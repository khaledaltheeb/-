# Rawafid Magazine — Scientific Quality Standard

Owner scope: `/magazine/` and all nested magazine research routes.

This standard is mandatory for every published research-analysis page. A page is not considered complete merely because it has a source URL or a long body. It must accurately interpret the cited source and provide a useful Arabic critical reading.

## 1. Source fidelity — mandatory

Every page must:

- identify the primary/original source with a stable URL (prefer DOI/publisher; add PubMed/PMC or registry when available);
- verify that the Arabic title and page topic actually match the source;
- classify the evidence correctly (systematic review, meta-analysis, RCT, cohort, qualitative study, thesis, guideline, protocol, etc.);
- preserve exact study identity: authors, journal/publisher, year, DOI/PMID/registry identifier when available;
- never attribute a finding to the source unless it is supported by the source;
- distinguish the study's own conclusions from Rawafid's interpretation.

## 2. Minimum scientific content — mandatory

Each page must contain, when applicable:

1. **Executive summary** — the main answer without exaggeration.
2. **Research question** — what the study actually tested.
3. **Design and methods** — design, databases/setting, eligibility, comparison, follow-up, analytic approach.
4. **Sample/evidence base** — participants/studies, relevant demographics and setting.
5. **Primary results** — exact numerical results when reported (effect sizes, CIs, event rates, sensitivity/specificity, etc.).
6. **Statistical versus practical/clinical meaning** — do not equate statistical significance with meaningful benefit.
7. **Risk of bias / evidence certainty** — RoB, GRADE, confidence/quality assessment, or the closest source-reported equivalent.
8. **Heterogeneity and sensitivity analyses** — when relevant to reviews/meta-analyses.
9. **Limitations** — explicit and source-grounded; include transferability/generalizability limits.
10. **What the study does NOT prove** — mandatory anti-overclaim section.
11. **Practical implications** — for families, schools, clinicians, institutions, or policy only to the degree supported by evidence.
12. **Decision/checklist section** — when it materially helps readers apply the evidence safely.
13. **FAQ** — only high-value questions; no filler or repetition.
14. **Primary source and references** — original source first, then authoritative supporting records.
15. **Limits of Rawafid's reading** — clarify that the page is an educational critical reading, not replacement for the original article or individual medical advice.

## 3. Quantitative integrity

For quantitative studies:

- use exact denominators and sample sizes;
- report absolute risks as well as relative measures when available and useful;
- include 95% confidence intervals for important effect estimates when reported;
- preserve sign/direction of standardized effects;
- never convert association into causation for observational designs;
- never describe subgroup findings as proof of superiority unless the design and interaction test support it;
- flag high heterogeneity and imprecision explicitly;
- do not infer absence of bias from a non-significant publication-bias test;
- **never infer, calculate, reconstruct, estimate, or invent a 95% CI, prediction interval, `I²`, GRADE/certainty rating, risk-of-bias judgment, p-value, sample denominator, or other uncertainty metric solely because the audit expects one**;
- when an important uncertainty, heterogeneity, or certainty measure is not visible in a verifiable final source, say explicitly that it was **not available/not verifiable in the source version reviewed**, identify which source version was checked, and leave the numerical field unset until it can be verified;
- distinguish **not reported**, **not performed**, and **not accessible in the reviewed source**. These are different scientific states and must not be collapsed into one another;
- if a trusted secondary evidence map or guideline reports a number that differs from the primary publisher/index record, surface the discrepancy and keep each value attributed to its source rather than silently choosing one.

## 4. Review/meta-analysis requirements

A systematic review or meta-analysis should normally state:

- number of included studies and participants;
- search scope/databases and date where available;
- included study designs;
- risk-of-bias method;
- pooled effect(s) and confidence intervals **when verifiably reported**;
- heterogeneity (`I²` or equivalent) **when verifiably reported and applicable**;
- sensitivity/subgroup/meta-regression findings only with appropriate caution;
- publication-bias assessment if performed;
- GRADE/certainty assessment if performed;
- limitations caused by diversity of interventions/outcomes/populations.

If a review is described as **without meta-analysis**, a scoping review, narrative synthesis, qualitative synthesis, evidence map, or another design that does not produce a pooled estimate, do not force meta-analysis fields into the page. If a meta-analysis exists but an accessible source does not expose a particular CI, `I²`, inconsistency statistic, or GRADE table, document the availability limitation instead of fabricating completeness.

## 5. Trial requirements

An RCT page should normally state:

- trial design and registration;
- sample size and allocation;
- comparator/control;
- primary outcome and time point;
- effect estimate with uncertainty;
- attrition/adherence when material;
- adverse events/safety when relevant;
- blinding limitations;
- whether analysis was intention-to-treat when reported;
- whether the finding is superiority, non-inferiority, equivalence, feasibility, or exploratory.

## 6. Observational-study requirements

Observational pages must:

- describe design (cohort, cross-sectional, case-control, registry, etc.);
- identify key confounders/adjustments;
- state that association does not prove causation;
- distinguish prevalence/incidence/risk/odds/hazard correctly;
- avoid clinical recommendations that exceed the design.

## 7. Arabic editorial quality

Every page must:

- use clear Modern Standard Arabic;
- define technical terms on first use when needed;
- avoid literal translation that distorts scientific meaning;
- avoid filler, generic advice, repeated paragraphs, and unsupported certainty;
- use consistent Arabic terminology while retaining the English acronym when it improves precision;
- keep numbers, units, drug names, genes, scales, and statistical symbols exact.

## 8. Safety and ethics

- No individual diagnosis or treatment recommendation from a single study.
- State when professional assessment is required.
- For suicide/self-harm, abuse, medication, oncology, pregnancy, pediatric, or other high-stakes topics, add explicit scope and safety boundaries.
- Protect privacy and avoid stigmatizing framing.
- Do not imply regulatory approval, guideline endorsement, or standard of care unless verified.

## 9. Metadata and provenance

Every published record should have:

- `content_type = research`;
- `status = published` only after quality review;
- `/magazine/` canonical URL;
- correct `schema_json.evidence_kind`;
- valid `schema_json.source_url` for the primary source;
- at least one valid original-source URL in `references_json`;
- useful excerpt;
- non-truncated SEO title and description;
- `last_reviewed_at` updated after substantive scientific review;
- references ordered with the primary source first.

## 10. Priority rules for legacy pages

Fix in this order:

1. wrong or generic evidence classification;
2. source mismatch or unverifiable source;
3. short legacy summaries with little numerical evidence;
4. missing limitations or methods;
5. overclaiming or causal language unsupported by design;
6. single-reference pages where authoritative source records can be added;
7. missing practical interpretation;
8. SEO/editorial polish only after scientific correctness.

## 11. Acceptance test

A magazine page passes only when all are true:

- [ ] source opened and identity verified;
- [ ] evidence type verified;
- [ ] central numbers checked against source;
- [ ] uncertainty/heterogeneity/certainty values are either source-verified or explicitly marked unavailable/not performed rather than inferred;
- [ ] research question and methods present;
- [ ] results interpreted without overclaiming;
- [ ] limitations explicit;
- [ ] `what this does not prove` present;
- [ ] practical implications are evidence-bounded;
- [ ] references valid and primary source first;
- [ ] metadata/SEO complete;
- [ ] Arabic copy is clear, useful, non-repetitive;
- [ ] high-stakes safety language added when needed;
- [ ] `last_reviewed_at` refreshed after review.

## 12. Operating rule

Do not increase magazine volume while critical legacy pages fail this standard. Prioritize repair of the weakest published pages, then maintain the same standard for all newly published pages.

## 13. Continuous audit gate

The repository includes `scripts/magazine-quality-audit.mjs`, which audits the **published production records** rather than a static content snapshot.

- Run `node scripts/magazine-quality-audit.mjs --strict-critical` to fail on critical structural/provenance defects.
- `.github/workflows/magazine-quality-audit.yml` runs the audit every day and on changes to the magazine quality system.
- Reports are retained as `magazine-quality-audit` workflow artifacts in JSON and Markdown.
- A green automated audit means no detected critical structural/provenance defect; it does **not** replace opening and scientifically reading the primary source.
- Source-level review remains the acceptance authority for factual accuracy, numerical interpretation, study design, risk of bias, and clinical or educational implications.
- An audit warning about a missing statistic is a retrieval/review task, **not permission to infer the statistic**. The source record always outranks the heuristic score.
