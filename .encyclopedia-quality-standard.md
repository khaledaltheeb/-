# Rawafid Encyclopedia Gold-Standard Contract

This contract governs published records whose canonical URL starts with `/encyclopedia/`.

## Purpose

The encyclopedia is not a dumping ground for short definitions and it is not a reason to make every page look the same. Each published entry must do one job clearly and truthfully: explain a condition or concept, or solve a focused support/access problem, while showing what is known and uncertain and preserving a traceable source trail.

**Content purpose outranks the legacy `content_type` label.** Some historical records are stored as `condition` even though their actual intent is educational support, school safety, classroom access or accommodation. Those pages must not be padded into duplicate disease monographs merely to satisfy a generic word floor.

Word count is therefore a regression floor inside a page-purpose contract, never a writing target.

## Gold-standard marker

A page enters the strict contract only when `schema_json.encyclopedia_gold_standard.version >= 1`.

The marker should declare `mode` explicitly for all new upgrades:

- `condition_reference` — the primary condition/disorder reference page;
- `specialized_support` — a focused educational, school, classroom, communication, participation, accommodation or safety page whose value is distinct from the primary condition reference;
- `glossary` — a concept/term page.

Wave 001 predates the explicit mode field. For backward compatibility a gold `condition` page with no mode is interpreted as `condition_reference`, while a gold `glossary_term` page with no mode is interpreted as `glossary`.

`evidence_limited=true` is an evidence-state modifier, not a page mode. It may lower a quantitative floor only when the page explicitly explains why the available evidence cannot support additional disease-specific detail.

Legacy or pre-upgrade pages are audited and queued but are not automatically unpublished. This prevents a new quality system from breaking thousands of valid existing routes while the backlog is being repaired.

## Mode 1 — condition reference

A gold-standard `condition_reference` page must have all of the following:

- one stable `/encyclopedia/<slug>/` canonical;
- a precise definition and diagnostic/genetic boundary appropriate to the condition;
- phenotype/clinical presentation with explicit variability and anti-overgeneralization language;
- diagnosis/testing or diagnostic reasoning appropriate to the evidence base;
- management/support written as evidence-led or symptom-directed, never as an invented disease-specific treatment;
- surveillance/reassessment or a clear statement that no disorder-specific surveillance guideline exists;
- functional, communication, educational, participation or accessibility implications when relevant;
- an explicit evidence-limits section for rare or incompletely characterized conditions;
- FAQ/search-intent coverage;
- a claim-to-source map in `schema_json.claim_source_map`;
- a real `last_reviewed_at` timestamp and truthful review provenance;
- no absolute cure, guarantee, unsupported causal claim or unsafe medication instruction.

### Quantitative floors

These are regression guards, not writing targets:

- useful rendered words: >= 1,200, unless `evidence_limited=true` and the page explicitly explains why the evidence base cannot support that depth;
- structured blocks: >= 20; evidence-limited condition references >= 14;
- valid reference URLs: >= 4 when four independent relevant sources exist; evidence-limited condition references may use >= 2 only with the explicit evidence-limit marker;
- claim-source mappings: >= 4;
- at least one authoritative clinical/genetic/official source;
- at least one source from 2024 or later when a relevant recent source exists.

## Mode 2 — specialized support

A `specialized_support` page has a deliberately narrower search intent. Examples include educational support for a named condition, school seizure safety, classroom hyperacusis, disability access, communication accommodations or condition-specific participation planning.

It must **not** recreate a full disease monograph when a primary condition reference already exists. Instead it should make its scope explicit and solve the focused problem better.

A gold specialized-support page should contain:

- a direct scope statement explaining what the page covers and what it does not replace;
- enough clinical/contextual definition to make the support safe, but no copied full diagnostic section solely to increase length;
- a functional-needs model: person × task × environment, or an equivalent evidence-led framework;
- practical supports/accommodations that are specific enough to apply and flexible enough to individualize;
- contraindications, safety/escalation boundaries or a clear statement when no special safety rule is required;
- a method to measure whether the support is working and when to reassess it;
- accessibility/participation implications where relevant;
- FAQ/search-intent coverage;
- claim-to-source mapping;
- real review provenance;
- direct linkage to the primary condition/reference page when an appropriate internal page exists.

### Quantitative floors

- useful rendered words: >= 650; >= 450 only when `evidence_limited=true` and the limitation is explicit;
- structured blocks: >= 12; evidence-limited specialized support >= 10;
- valid references: >= 3; evidence-limited support >= 2;
- claim-source mappings: >= 3;
- at least one authoritative clinical/professional/official source;
- at least one current source from 2024 or later when a relevant current source exists.

A support page is allowed to be shorter than a condition reference because **distinct utility and non-duplication are quality requirements**.

## Mode 3 — glossary

A gold-standard `glossary` / `glossary_term` page should normally contain:

- a direct definition;
- what the term does and does not mean;
- context of use;
- interpretation pitfalls;
- practical or methodological implications;
- at least two traceable references;
- review provenance;
- no pseudo-diagnostic wording when the entry is a concept rather than a diagnosis.

The strict useful-content floor is 350 rendered words for glossary terms unless the entry is inherently atomic and its schema records `evidence_limited=true`.

## Legacy-purpose inference

Purpose inference is used only to **prioritize the repair backlog**; it never grants a page a gold marker automatically.

A legacy `condition` record is treated as a likely `specialized_support` page only when the title, English term or canonical explicitly expresses a focused support intent such as education, school support, classroom access or accommodations. Generic mentions of education or support inside body text or `page_mechanism.purpose` are not sufficient, because true condition references should also discuss function and participation.

Examples of strong support-intent signals include slugs/titles such as:

- `*-education` / `دعم التعليم ...`;
- `*-school-support` / `دعم ... في المدرسة`;
- `*-classroom` / `... في الصف`;
- `*-school-accommodations` / `تكييفات ... في المدرسة`.

A record such as `auditory-processing-disorder` or `cluttering` remains a condition/concept candidate unless its canonical intent itself is specialized, even if its existing page mechanism mentions practical support.

## Source quality

Source count alone does not establish quality. Prefer the strongest source appropriate to the claim:

1. current official guideline, GeneReviews/NCBI clinical reference, ClinGen expert curation, WHO/NICE/CDC or equivalent authoritative body;
2. systematic review/meta-analysis or major consensus statement;
3. recent peer-reviewed cohort or primary study when the condition is too rare for higher-level evidence;
4. high-quality rare-disease reference such as Orphanet when it adds identity/phenotype context;
5. professional education/accessibility standards for the education/access claims they actually support.

Do not cite a general inclusion source as evidence for disease-specific clinical facts. Conversely, do not use a genetics paper as the only support for a classroom accommodation claim when a professional accessibility or education source is available.

## Review provenance

A review badge may be shown only when the database has a real review timestamp. The existing `contentReviewProvenance` behavior remains authoritative: no reviewer attribution is manufactured.

## Backlog policy

Existing published encyclopedia pages stay live unless they contain a safety-critical defect. The audit creates a prioritized repair queue using:

- unsafe or contradictory medical claims;
- missing or invalid references;
- low depth **relative to the page's purpose**;
- absent diagnosis/testing boundaries on condition references;
- absent practical support/measurement boundaries on specialized-support pages;
- absent evidence limitations for rare or incompletely characterized conditions;
- stale or missing review provenance;
- missing claim-source mapping;
- incomplete reference metadata.

The repair strategy is in-place enrichment. Do not create duplicate pages or change canonicals to work around quality gaps. When a support page overlaps a condition reference, sharpen the support page's intent and cross-link the two instead of merging both into duplicate long-form articles.
