## Legacy/history audit

The current legacy page was inspected together with its Git history. The page was published as a Quick Information child/growth item and later received broad batch changes to long-form enrichment, platform shell, truthful modification metadata, GA4/GTM and Discover/sitemap layers. These later commits were treated as shell/operational revisions rather than separate knowledge canonicals.

Useful legacy intent retained: fear of punishment, approval seeking, fantasy/reality in younger children, adult modeling, and the possibility that a child may be protecting a person or unsafe secret. Removed rather than migrated: generic emergency boilerplate, repeated diagnostic disclaimers, agent/QA-style scaffolding, and irrelevant structured-data citations to ADHD/autism that did not support the lying topic.

## Rebuild and evidence

The page was rebuilt from scratch around developmental context, functions of lying, privacy, homework/task avoidance, adult modeling, calm accountability, repair, school response, social/polite lies, safeguarding, and criteria for professional evaluation. Evidence was checked against authoritative/professional sources and research indexed by PubMed, including AAP/HealthyChildren, AACAP, developmental deception reviews, studies of punitive environments and honesty appeals, modeling research, positive-discipline guidance, and NSPCC safeguarding guidance.

The page does not label a child as a “liar,” does not present lying as a diagnosis, and distinguishes ordinary behavioral guidance from safeguarding situations. A disclosure or unsafe secret is handled as a child-protection concern, not as a truth-telling exercise.
