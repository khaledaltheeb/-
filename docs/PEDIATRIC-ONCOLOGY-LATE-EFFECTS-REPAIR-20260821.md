## Hearing / ototoxicity

The hearing guide now separates during-treatment ototoxicity monitoring from long-term survivor surveillance. It is exposure-led and covers platinum chemotherapy, head/brain radiotherapy, tinnitus, age-appropriate audiology, school/language effects, assistive listening technology, unilateral/high-frequency loss, and transition to adult care.

The page explicitly limits sodium thiosulfate to clinician-led otoprotection in selected cisplatin contexts; it is not presented as a home treatment or as treatment for established hearing loss.

Key evidence families include IGHG ototoxicity surveillance, SIOP during-treatment audiologic monitoring, pediatric cisplatin otoprotection guidance, recent survivor communication/academic outcome reviews, and NCI late-effects guidance.

## Thyroid late effects

The thyroid guide separates thyroid-function surveillance from thyroid-nodule/cancer surveillance. It covers primary versus central hypothyroidism, TSH plus free T4 interpretation, radiation/TBI/MIBG exposures, growth and puberty interactions, nodules, transition, pregnancy considerations, and medication-safety boundaries.

A key scientific controversy is preserved rather than flattened: IGHG/PanCare does not establish ultrasound as universally superior to neck palpation in overall benefit-harm balance and supports shared decision making, while newer institutional studies and the 2026 ISPED position paper support more active ultrasound surveillance in selected exposed survivors. The page presents this as an evolving policy area rather than a universal ultrasound rule.

## Pulmonary late effects

The pulmonary guide follows the current IGHG surveillance position: strong awareness of pulmonary symptoms, with pulmonary-function testing for symptomatic survivors rather than universal routine PFT screening of every asymptomatic at-risk survivor because evidence and actionable-intervention benefit remain limited.

It also explains that HSCT programs and survivors with established pulmonary disease can require more intensive specialist surveillance. Recent ALL-STAR Lungs data are used to support tailored high-risk monitoring without overstating observational evidence as proof that universal screening improves outcomes.

The page includes thoracic radiation, surgery, HSCT, bleomycin and other pulmonary-toxic exposures, spirometry/lung volumes/DLCO, exercise, differential diagnosis, anesthesia history, and emergency respiratory red flags.

## Growth / growth-hormone deficiency

The growth guide now distinguishes short stature from confirmed growth-hormone deficiency and centers longitudinal growth velocity. It covers hypothalamic-pituitary radiation/surgery, TBI, direct spinal irradiation, thyroid and pubertal disorders, nutrition, IGF-1 limitations, stimulation-testing boundaries, bone age, and transition to adult endocrinology.

Growth-hormone replacement is presented only for appropriately selected survivors with confirmed deficiency and no active malignancy, coordinated between endocrinology and oncology. Current consensus evidence is described as generally reassuring for primary-cancer recurrence risk, while acknowledging the complexity of second-neoplasm data and the dominant role of prior treatment exposures such as cranial radiotherapy.

## Release posture

These guides are editorially and mechanically repaired, not medically released. Publication still requires an independent qualified reviewer, the existing pediatric-oncology release guard, and route verification after the publication phase transition.
