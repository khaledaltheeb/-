## 1. WAGR spectrum disorder

The page now treats WAGR as an 11p13 deletion spectrum rather than a fixed four-feature acronym.

Key points:

- molecular boundaries and the roles of `WT1` and `PAX6` are separated from phenotype prediction;
- Wilms tumor surveillance is distinguished from lifelong renal monitoring;
- aniridia/visual access planning is integrated with school participation;
- genitourinary, developmental, metabolic/BDNF-related and neurobehavioral variability are not overgeneralized;
- surveillance remains specialist-led rather than converted into a self-executed schedule.

Primary anchor: current GeneReviews WAGR Spectrum Disorder, supported by NIH/MedlinePlus and relevant clinical references.

## 2. MID1-related Opitz G/BBB syndrome

The prior title and content could blur historically different entities. The page now explicitly centers `MID1`-related X-linked Opitz G/BBB syndrome.

Key corrections:

- airway, laryngotracheoesophageal, swallowing, palatal and genitourinary manifestations are prioritized where clinically relevant;
- developmental and communication outcomes are presented as variable;
- historical autosomal-dominant “Opitz G/BBB” labeling is not treated as genetically interchangeable with MID1-related disease; SPECC1L-related craniofacial disorder is separated;
- feeding/airway safety and specialist assessment outrank generic educational advice.

## 3. Waardenburg syndrome

The page now treats Waardenburg syndrome as a genetically and phenotypically heterogeneous group.

Evidence anchors include GeneReviews, NIH/MedlinePlus, a 2024 genetic-diagnosis study, a 2026 systematic review and current CDC early-hearing guidance.

Key corrections:

- the phenotype is not inferred from pigmentation alone;
- hearing can be normal or affected to very different degrees by subtype and individual;
- genes such as `PAX3`, `MITF`, `SOX10`, `EDN3`, `EDNRB`, `SNAI2` and `KITLG` are kept within the appropriate heterogeneous framework;
- a VUS is not treated as a confirmed diagnosis;
- early language access is the goal, not compulsory selection of one communication modality or hearing technology;
- hearing devices are evaluated through functional benefit and participation, not device use alone;
- multilingual families are not advised to abandon the home language;
- type-IV/Hirschsprung considerations are separated from the rest of the spectrum;
- adult/transition, emergency communication and school-access planning are included.

## 4. Potocki-Shaffer syndrome

This record is explicitly `evidence_limited=true` because the syndrome is exceptionally rare and much of the management literature comes from small case series.

Evidence anchors include current NIH/MedlinePlus, Orphanet, the 2020 literature review, the 2010 multidisciplinary management series, and GeneReviews context for osteochondromas.

Key corrections:

- the 11p11.2 deletion is interpreted as a contiguous-gene disorder;
- `ALX4`, `EXT2` and `PHF21A` roles are described without assuming they explain every feature;
- deletion size matters but does not deterministically predict function;
- parietal foramina and osteochondromas are translated into risk-based activity and orthopedic decisions rather than blanket restrictions;
- persistent pain, rapid new growth or neurovascular symptoms around an osteochondroma are escalation signals;
- epilepsy/early-spasm discussion is retained as limited evidence rather than a universal screening claim;
- hearing, vision, renal/cardiac/urinary findings are treated as subset findings, not mandatory features;
- no universal surveillance timetable is invented;
- AAC, functional assessment and educational participation are integrated without lowering expectations.

## 5. Childhood Apraxia of Speech (CAS) canonical consolidation

### Problem found

The current site contained multiple indexable CAS pages with overlapping diagnostic/reference intent:

- `/encyclopedia/childhood-apraxia-speech/`
- `/content/special-ed-encyclopedia-childhood-apraxia-of-speech`
- `/special-needs/communication/childhood-apraxia-of-speech/`

At the same time, three other routes were genuinely differentiated and therefore should **not** be consolidated:

- `/capabilities/childhood-apraxia-of-speech/` — strengths/access/function pathway;
- `/outside-the-box/childhood-apraxia-of-speech/` — experimental/functional pathway;
- `/encyclopedia/childhood-apraxia-speech-school-support/` — focused school-support entry.

### Decision

`/encyclopedia/childhood-apraxia-speech/` is the single core diagnostic/reference owner.

The owner inherited the strongest existing Rawafid scientific CAS reference and was refreshed with:

- ASHA Practice Portal;
- 2026 scoping review showing there is no error-free diagnostic gold standard;
- 2024 treatment review;
- 2026 CAS-specific AAC scoping review;
- 2024 systematic scoping review of literacy;
- existing NIDCD/Cochrane/ICF/inclusion sources.

Key corrections:

- no single sign, checklist or automated score is described as diagnostic;
- diagnosis requires motor-speech assessment and differential diagnosis by an experienced SLP;
- motor-based treatment is presented as individualized; no single brand/program is declared universally superior;
- AAC is a complementary early communication option, not a last resort;
- literacy risk is assessed proactively rather than waiting for later academic failure;
- multilingual children retain access to home languages;
- progress requires generalization to untrained words, listeners and real communication.
