## 1. Pica

The Gold reference now treats pica as a combined medical-safety and behavioral problem rather than merely unusual eating behavior.

Key corrections:

- normal oral exploration and culturally accepted practices are separated from disorder-level pica;
- risk is stratified by ingested material: toxicity, obstruction, infection, dental injury and nutritional complications;
- iron/zinc deficiency is an association to assess, not a universal causal explanation;
- potential lead exposure has a CDC-based safety pathway, including clinically silent childhood exposure and blood testing;
- urgent warning signs and dangerous ingestions are separated from routine behavioral management;
- developmental disability/autism context is included without normalizing the medical risk;
- functional behavioral assessment identifies maintaining variables;
- least-restrictive environmental, discrimination and reinforcement strategies are prioritized;
- old aversive/punishment literature is not converted into a home or school instruction;
- school/environmental safety plans and measurable outcomes are included;
- a dedicated **follow-up and reassessment** layer now reviews material type, exposure frequency, medical results, environmental safety, behavioral response and whether supervision can be safely changed.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR, MedlinePlus, CDC lead guidance, the systematic review of behavioral pica treatment and a 2024 behavioral assessment/treatment report.

## 2. Rumination disorder / rumination syndrome

The page explicitly prevents a major Arabic-language ambiguity: **food rumination is not cognitive rumination/overthinking**.

Key corrections:

- `rumination syndrome` and `rumination disorder` terminology is explained across gastroenterology and feeding/eating classifications;
- characteristic post-prandial, often effortless regurgitation is described without making it a self-test;
- GERD, vomiting, achalasia/esophageal disorders, gastroparesis and eating-disorder differential diagnosis are included;
- clinical history is central; specialized post-prandial manometry/impedance supports selected uncertain cases rather than every patient;
- diaphragmatic breathing is the core first-line competing response, not generic relaxation advice;
- biofeedback, CBT-informed and multidisciplinary behavioral treatment are included when simple instruction is insufficient;
- pediatric evidence limitations from the 2024 systematic review are explicit;
- nutrition, growth, dental health, social eating and school participation are tracked;
- variability in age at onset, episode frequency, associated GI symptoms and treatment response is now explicit;
- no medication dose or self-directed pharmacologic protocol is provided.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR, AGA clinical review, 2024 current-state review, 2024 pediatric systematic review, 2024 multidisciplinary treatment study and 2024 prevalence meta-analysis.

## 3. Persistent Depressive Disorder

The page no longer treats persistent depression as merely “long-lasting bad mood.”

Key corrections:

- duration is context but is not sufficient by itself for diagnosis;
- chronic symptoms can be under-recognized when patients normalize them as personality;
- suicide thoughts remain a direct safety issue even when longstanding;
- bipolar history, medical causes, sleep, substances and medications are part of differential assessment;
- chronic depressive symptoms and episodes of major depression can overlap;
- CBT addresses avoidance, rumination and interpersonal maintaining processes rather than generic positive thinking;
- antidepressant treatment is a shared clinical decision without dose or switching instructions;
- combined therapy is a reasoned option, not automatic escalation;
- initial non-response prompts diagnostic, adherence, comorbidity and treatment-strategy review rather than therapeutic nihilism;
- work, school, social participation and measurable function are part of recovery;
- variability in symptom course and treatment response is explicit so the diagnosis does not imply one fixed severity or one universal plan.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR, NIMH persistent-depression and depression resources, NICE NG222 (current review 2026), and the 2025 systematic review of treatment-response predictors in persisting depression.

## 4. Acute Stress Disorder

The page protects against over-medicalizing normal early post-trauma reactions while identifying clinically important acute post-traumatic syndromes.

Key corrections:

- intense distress in the first 72 hours is not automatically ASD;
- the commonly used DSM timing frame of three days to one month is explained without pretending time alone makes a diagnosis;
- ASD is not a deterministic predictor that PTSD will follow;
- injury, delirium, substances, pain, panic, depression, dissociation and other medical/psychiatric explanations are included;
- immediate priorities are safety, shelter, physical care, social support and practical information;
- WHO Psychological First Aid is humane, practical and non-coercive support;
- forced retelling and universal Critical Incident Stress Debriefing are not routine prevention for everyone exposed to trauma;
- stepped care and trauma-focused cognitive-behavioral treatment are used for clinically significant persistent early symptoms rather than every exposed person;
- no universal prophylactic medication recommendation or dose is given;
- children, school/work return, substance use, sleep and emergency safety are included;
- symptoms persisting beyond the first month trigger reassessment, including PTSD and other diagnoses.

Evidence anchors: VA National Center for PTSD ASD guidance updated 2026, VA/DoD PTSD-ASD CPG and 2024 synopsis, WHO Psychological First Aid, and APA assessment resources.

## 5. Intermittent Explosive Disorder

The Gold page separates IED from ordinary anger and from planned violence, and explicitly prevents diagnosis from becoming an excuse for abuse.

Key corrections:

- anger itself is not a diagnosis;
- recurrent disproportionate **impulsive** aggressive outbursts are distinguished from planned, instrumental or coercive aggression;
- bipolar disorder, substances, neurological injury/disease, developmental conditions, trauma and other psychiatric causes are part of differential assessment;
- childhood tantrums are not automatically IED;
- imminent violence, weapons, serious threats and vulnerable household members trigger a safety pathway before therapy;
- victim and child safety is independent of the aggressor's diagnosis;
- domestic violence is not reframed as illness that a victim must tolerate;
- CBT uses early-cue recognition, arousal regulation, cognitive/behavioral alternatives, problem-solving and relapse prevention;
- medication is an individualized specialist option, not a universal prescription;
- alcohol/substance use, sleep, pain and possible brain injury are assessed;
- pre-outburst plans, post-outburst accountability, school/work plans and measurable outcomes are included;
- a dedicated **follow-up and reassessment** layer reopens differential diagnosis and level of care when episode pattern, danger, bipolar/neurological signals or treatment response changes.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR, Mayo clinical references, and 2025 systematic reviews/meta-analyses of IED treatment, prevalence/factors and etiology/neurobiology.

## Strict audit correction

The first Wave 005 strict production audit was intentionally allowed to fail rather than weakening the quality gate.

It identified two critical wording/structure gaps:

- `pica`: the page measured outcomes but did not explicitly name a follow-up/reassessment layer;
- `intermittent-explosive-disorder`: the page measured outcomes but did not explicitly name follow-up/reassessment.

It also exposed two non-critical anti-overgeneralization warnings:

- `persistent-depressive-disorder`: course/response variability needed to be explicit;
- `rumination-disorder`: clinical and treatment-response variability needed to be explicit.

The pages were corrected in production. The audit gate was **not** relaxed. The final metrics table above reflects the corrected structured content.
