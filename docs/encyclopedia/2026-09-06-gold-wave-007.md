## Conduct disorder

The former short reference is now a child/young-person safety and treatment reference rather than a behavior label.

Major upgrades:

- a single fight, lie or rule violation is explicitly insufficient for diagnosis;
- safety and safeguarding come before diagnostic completion when weapons, serious threats, firesetting, sexual violence, animal cruelty, exploitation, suicidality or inability to protect others are present;
- information is gathered across child, family, school and relevant services rather than from one complainant;
- ODD, ADHD, autism/developmental needs, learning problems, trauma, depression, substances and environmental adversity are separated from or integrated into the differential;
- limited prosocial emotions/callous-unemotional features are treated as a clinical specifier, not a deterministic label such as “future psychopath”;
- punishment-only approaches are rejected as treatment; clear safe consequences are distinguished from humiliation or violence;
- parent/caregiver interventions and multicomponent psychosocial treatments are core;
- multimodal/multisystemic/community approaches are represented for some adolescents with severe conduct problems;
- school participation, bullying, learning and educational exclusion are integrated into the plan;
- medication is not presented as routine treatment of conduct problems themselves; comorbid ADHD and other conditions retain their own treatment pathways;
- outcome monitoring includes safety, aggression/property violations, school attendance, substances, peers, learning and victim/child injury;
- the course is explicitly non-deterministic.

Evidence anchors include WHO ICD-11 CDDR, NICE CG158/current quality-standard material, the 2025 AHRQ disruptive-behavior systematic review, a 2026 Pediatrics meta-analysis of psychosocial interventions, a 2026 evidence-based psychotherapy review, and 2024 conduct-disorder comorbidity/risk-factor evidence.

## Schizophreniform disorder

This page is now a DSM-specific time-course and first-episode psychosis reference.

Major upgrades:

- `schizophreniform disorder` is explicitly identified as a DSM diagnosis; it is not falsely mapped one-to-one to ICD-11;
- the shorter duration is not described as milder illness;
- first-episode psychosis receives urgent specialist assessment/treatment without waiting for the final label;
- substances/medications, medical causes and mood disorders are part of the differential;
- brief psychotic disorder and schizophrenia are separated by DSM course while avoiding a simplistic severity ladder;
- attenuated/high-risk states are not mislabeled as established psychosis;
- recent longitudinal first-episode data are used to explain why this initial diagnosis can be highly unstable;
- early psychosis care includes specialist medication management, CBT/family intervention and functional recovery rather than medication alone;
- cannabis/substance use, sleep, education/work, side effects and relapse warning signs are included;
- diagnostic reassessment is treated as part of the diagnosis itself;
- disorder-specific treatment evidence is explicitly described as limited and often extrapolated from first-episode psychosis/schizophrenia care.

## Brief psychotic disorder

The page now separates DSM `Brief Psychotic Disorder` from ICD-11 `Acute and Transient Psychotic Disorder` rather than treating the labels as synonyms.

Major upgrades:

- acute safety and first-episode psychosis pathways come before final nomenclature;
- medical, neurological, substance/medication and mood causes are actively considered;
- marked stress can precede an episode but is not required and does not prove a purely psychological cause;
- duration differentiates DSM categories but does not create a severity ladder;
- pooled recurrence estimates are explicitly labeled as estimates from mixed “brief psychotic episode” research constructs, not individual BPD probabilities;
- recent first-episode data and prior meta-analysis support longitudinal diagnostic reassessment;
- acute treatment includes safety, psychosocial support and specialist psychosis treatment; no self-directed antipsychotic dose/duration is provided;
- inpatient versus intensive community care is based on risk and function, not the diagnostic name alone;
- CBT/family work, sleep, substances, study/work and relapse planning are included;
- the evidence limitation created by pooling DSM BPD, ICD ATPD and other brief-psychosis constructs is explicit.

## Schizoaffective disorder

The page now centers the longitudinal psychosis-mood relationship rather than simply “psychosis plus depression/mania.”

Major upgrades:

- one visit with psychosis plus mood symptoms is explicitly insufficient;
- DSM and ICD-11 are both acknowledged but not falsely treated as operationally identical;
- the longitudinal relationship with schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and major depression with psychotic features is explicit;
- substances, medications and medical causes remain in the differential;
- suicide/violence/self-care/catatonia and severe mania/depression safety are included;
- the historically lower diagnostic reliability and continuing conceptual controversy are stated, based on systematic and 2024 review evidence;
- treatment targets psychosis, the relevant mood syndrome, psychological needs and community/function support rather than one fixed drug recipe;
- CBT evidence from mixed schizophrenia/schizoaffective samples is not misrepresented as schizoaffective-specific efficacy;
- physical health and treatment adverse-effect monitoring, sleep, substances, work/study and family/community support are included;
- psychosis and mood outcomes are measured separately;
- diagnosis is re-opened after major new episodes when the longitudinal relationship changes.

## Delusional disorder

The page is now a culturally and clinically safe reality-assessment reference with deliberately modest treatment claims.

Major upgrades:

- an unusual belief is not labeled a delusion merely because a clinician finds it implausible;
- culturally/religiously shared beliefs and cultural syndromes require contextual assessment;
- reports of stalking, abuse, infidelity, poisoning or medical illness are not automatically dismissed as delusions; verifiable real-world claims remain open to investigation;
- schizophrenia, OCD/poor-insight presentations, illness anxiety/BDD, dementia, delirium, neurological disease, substances/medications and mood disorders are considered;
- risk assessment focuses on what the person intends to do in response to the belief, not on stigma from the diagnosis;
- delusional jealousy is used only as a specific safety context; case-literature violence estimates are not generalized to all patients;
- communication avoids ridicule and also avoids automatic validation of an unverified delusion;
- therapeutic alliance, psychotherapy and functional goals are included;
- antipsychotics are described as an individualized specialist option while explicitly stating that the disorder-specific evidence is too weak to declare one universally preferred drug;
- treatment limitations are made explicit because much of the literature is case-series/cohort level;
- outcome monitoring includes distress, preoccupation, confrontations, sleep, safety, work/relationships and flexibility rather than requiring the patient to verbally renounce the belief.

## Cross-wave quality and safety rules

- No self-diagnosis shortcut.
- No medication dose, taper, switch or self-prescribing protocol.
- Psychosis first presentations retain medical/substance/mood differential diagnosis and urgent safety pathways.
- DSM/ICD nomenclature differences are explicit where material.
- Pooled outcome data are not presented as individual prognostic probabilities.
- Conduct disorder is not framed as criminality or a deterministic precursor to antisocial personality disorder.
- Delusional disorder does not become a tool for dismissing culture, religion, abuse or other potentially real claims.
- No external clinical review, partner approval or newly invented review timestamp is claimed.
