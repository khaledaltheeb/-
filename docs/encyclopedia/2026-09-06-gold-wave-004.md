## 1. Catatonia

The prior page was a short definition. The new reference is safety-led and corrects three high-risk misconceptions:

- catatonia is not synonymous with schizophrenia;
- mutism/immobility are not assumed to be deliberate refusal;
- malignant catatonia or a dangerous medical mimic must not be delayed as a behavioral issue.

The page now covers:

- psychomotor signs and structured observation;
- malignant catatonia and urgent escalation;
- complications of immobility, hydration/nutrition, thrombosis, pressure injury and aspiration;
- differential diagnosis including delirium, non-convulsive status epilepticus, medication syndromes and encephalitis;
- the role and limitations of rating scales and benzodiazepine challenge;
- evidence-led roles of benzodiazepines and ECT without dose/self-treatment instructions;
- caution around antipsychotic use during active catatonia;
- neurodevelopmental presentations and change-from-baseline logic;
- discharge, relapse and return-to-school/work planning.

Evidence anchors include WHO ICD-11 CDDR, the British Association for Psychopharmacology evidence-based consensus guideline, a 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis and neurodevelopmental consensus context.

## 2. Functional Neurological Disorder (FND)

The core correction is diagnostic framing: FND is not defined by “normal tests” or by psychological trauma.

The page now covers:

- positive neurological signs and why they support diagnosis;
- examples such as Hoover sign and tremor entrainment as clinician-interpreted signs, not patient self-tests;
- coexistence of FND with structural neurological disease;
- functional weakness, movement/gait symptoms, functional seizures and sensory/speech/visual symptoms;
- clear separation from malingering/factitious disorder;
- diagnosis communication as part of treatment engagement;
- FND-specific physiotherapy principles and automatic-movement retraining;
- occupational/psychological care where relevant;
- functional seizure safety and the danger of stopping antiseizure medication without specialist confirmation;
- symptom chronicity as a prognostic factor, not a reason to deny rehabilitation;
- work, school, driving/occupational safety and relapse planning.

Evidence anchors include WHO ICD-11, a recent treatment-outcomes meta-analysis, functional-motor physiotherapy consensus recommendations and a practical clinical review.

## 3. ARFID

The page now separates ARFID from normal selective eating, Pediatric Feeding Disorder, dysphagia, gastrointestinal disease and medically necessary dietary restriction.

Key changes:

- low BMI is explicitly **not required** for clinically important nutritional or functional risk;
- three common maintaining profiles are described without forcing people into fixed subtypes: sensory sensitivity, fear of aversive consequences and low interest/appetite;
- growth trajectory is used rather than a single percentile;
- medical stability, hydration, nutrient risk and refeeding risk are separated from psychiatric diagnosis;
- swallowing red flags trigger feeding/swallowing evaluation rather than being assumed to be fear alone;
- 2025 consensus on PFD/ARFID overlap is integrated;
- gastrointestinal disease and ARFID can coexist and require integrated care;
- autism/ADHD associations are included without using neurodevelopmental diagnosis to dismiss nutritional risk;
- treatment is multidisciplinary and non-coercive, with gradual exposure only when appropriate and medically safe;
- family, school, adult-transition and level-of-care planning are included;
- outcomes include nutrition, growth, supplement dependence, fear, flexibility and social participation.

Evidence anchors include NIMH, NICE NG69, the 2025 PFD/ARFID consensus, a 2026 ARFID/GI systematic review, an autism/ARFID meta-analysis and ASHA feeding/swallowing guidance.

## 4. Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD)

The core safety/editorial correction is preventing normal grief from being pathologized by duration alone.

The page now:

- explains the central grief pattern, functional impairment and cultural context;
- explicitly distinguishes ICD-11 and DSM-5-TR timing conventions;
- separates PGD from major depression, PTSD and adjustment disorder while allowing comorbidity;
- includes cultural/religious formulation, identity, meaning, avoidance and grief trajectories;
- differentiates universal bereavement support from targeted PGD treatment;
- presents grief-focused psychotherapy as the evidence-led intervention without implying that treatment means forgetting the deceased;
- does not present medication as a specific standalone treatment for the core PGD syndrome;
- covers children/adolescents, traumatic death, work/school, anniversaries, digital memories and family support;
- treats suicidal thoughts or a wish to die as a direct safety-assessment issue rather than dismissing them as normal bereavement.

Evidence anchors include WHO ICD-11, the American Psychiatric Association PGD resource, a 2024 World Psychiatry clinical review, a recent psychotherapy systematic review and a 2026 systematic review of bereavement care.

## 5. Hoarding Disorder

The encyclopedia page is now the diagnostic/treatment/safety owner. The existing Rawafid family guide remains a differentiated practical communication pathway:

- `/care-guides/hoarding-family-communication-safety-plan/`

The upgraded reference now covers:

- diagnostic distinction from collecting and from clutter caused by other conditions;
- excessive acquisition and variable insight;
- OCD/ADHD and cognitive/neurological differential context;
- fire, falls, blocked exits, sanitation, access to essential rooms/medicines and safeguarding;
- a harm-reduction hierarchy prioritizing life/safety/function before aesthetic order;
- why surprise/forced clean-outs are not treatment in the absence of an immediate safety requirement;
- specialized CBT components: motivation, decision-making, sorting/discarding, acquisition reduction and graded exposure to discarding distress;
- family accommodation and boundary setting;
- rights, capacity, self-neglect and local safeguarding/legal boundaries;
- older adults, animal-hoarding complexity, housing risk and relapse prevention;
- outcomes based on functional space, safety, acquisition, decision skill and quality of life rather than bags removed.

Evidence anchors include current NHS guidance, Oxford Health and South London and Maudsley specialist resources, a recent systematic review of patient perspectives, NHS self-neglect guidance and current ADHD/hoarding comorbidity evidence.
