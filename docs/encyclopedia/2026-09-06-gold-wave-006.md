## 1. Dissociative amnesia

The page is now a differential-diagnosis and memory-safety reference rather than a short trauma-memory definition.

Key corrections:

- clinically important autobiographical memory loss is separated from ordinary forgetting;
- neurological, head-injury, seizure, substance, medication, sleep and other medical explanations are explicitly considered before psychiatric attribution;
- urgent neurological/medical warning signs take priority over psychological interpretation;
- trauma history is context, not proof that a specific gap is a repressed memory;
- the page explicitly avoids forced or suggestive memory-recovery practices;
- vividness or late recovery of a memory is not treated as proof of historical truth or falsity;
- treatment focuses on safety, stability, functioning and associated symptoms rather than recovering every missing memory;
- practical support for medication management, appointments, work/education and temporary safety restrictions is included;
- reassessment reopens the differential if new neurological signs, new gaps or an atypical course emerge;
- the controversy and limitations of the recovered-memory evidence are explicit.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR (2024), MSD Manual Professional (2025), 2024 cognitive-memory reviews on repression/recovered memory, and the 2025 scoping review of recovery/retraction of abuse memories.

## 2. Illness anxiety disorder

The page now separates valid medical care from the checking/reassurance cycle that can maintain health anxiety.

Key corrections:

- the diagnosis does not mean symptoms are imaginary and does not exclude genuine physical disease;
- illness anxiety and real medical illness can coexist;
- the checking/search/reassurance and avoidance cycles are explained functionally;
- somatic symptom disorder, OCD, panic disorder, generalized anxiety and depression are included in differential assessment;
- new or important medical warning signs remain a medical pathway rather than being dismissed as anxiety;
- coordinated primary medical follow-up is preferred to unstructured repeated reassurance and doctor-shopping;
- CBT, exposure/response prevention, cognitive restructuring and mindfulness components are represented according to current evidence;
- structured internet-delivered CBT is distinguished from generic health information;
- medication remains an individualized clinician decision with no dose or self-directed switching protocol;
- family support is separated from endless reassurance;
- outcome measurement includes checking, reassurance, health searching, avoidance and return to work/sleep/relationships;
- pediatric evidence and cross-diagnostic treatment-study limitations are explicit.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR (2024), the 2024 Illness Anxiety Disorder review, the 2026 health-anxiety network meta-analysis, a 2025 CBT trial secondary analysis, and the interoception systematic review/meta-analysis.

## 3. Trichotillomania / hair-pulling disorder

The page now treats recurrent hair pulling as a measurable body-focused repetitive behavior with dermatologic and gastrointestinal safety boundaries.

Key corrections:

- focused and relatively automatic pulling are distinguished;
- dermatologic and medical causes of hair loss must be considered when the pattern is unclear;
- shame, blame and punishment are explicitly rejected as treatment mechanisms;
- trichophagia/hair swallowing and possible gastrointestinal complications are included;
- skin, eyelid and eye-region safety are included;
- Habit Reversal Training (HRT) is described as awareness training, competing response and context/stimulus work rather than simply “keeping the hands busy”;
- ACT-enhanced HRT is represented without overstating superiority;
- medication/supplement evidence is not converted into self-treatment instructions;
- child/family support is non-punitive and developmentally appropriate;
- school/work participation and privacy are included;
- outcomes include pulling episodes, urge, damage, strategy use and participation rather than hair count alone;
- follow-up reopens the plan if pulling shifts site, trichophagia emerges or a skin disorder appears.

Evidence anchors: MedlinePlus, NHS/SLaM clinical guidance, the 2026 treatment meta-analysis, and the 2024 genetics systematic review.

## 4. Body dysmorphic disorder

The page is now the diagnostic owner for BDD while the existing `/evidence-guides/body-dysmorphic-disorder-safe-guide/` remains the applied evidence guide.

Key corrections:

- ordinary appearance dissatisfaction is separated from clinically impairing preoccupation plus repetitive checking/comparison/camouflaging/reassurance behavior;
- variability in insight is explained without automatically reframing strongly held appearance beliefs as a separate psychotic disorder;
- eating disorders, OCD, social anxiety and dermatologic/medical conditions are part of differential assessment;
- self-harm/suicide safety assessment is explicit when risk indicators are present;
- cosmetic procedures are not presented as the default treatment for active BDD;
- the page deliberately avoids the opposite overclaim that cosmetic procedures can never help any selected patient;
- current aesthetic-surgery evidence is framed as heterogeneous and dependent on screening, severity, expectations and patient selection;
- BDD-tailored CBT, exposure/response prevention and reduction of checking/reassurance are central;
- current 2024–2026 meta-analyses and network meta-analysis support CBT while preserving uncertainty and heterogeneity;
- digital CBT is represented as promising structured treatment, not generic self-help;
- medication is a clinician/shared-decision option without dosing instructions;
- work, study, relationships and recovered participation are measurable outcomes;
- reassessment covers migration of appearance concern, repeated cosmetic requests, comorbidity and safety risk.

Evidence anchors: NHS (current), NICE CG31, 2024 CBT meta-analysis, 2024 aesthetic/reconstructive surgery systematic review/meta-analysis, and two 2026 psychotherapy meta-analytic reviews including the network meta-analysis.

PubMed titles were rechecked before release; abbreviated internal titles were corrected to the exact published titles for the BDD psychotherapy, CBT and aesthetic-surgery reviews.

## 5. Separation anxiety disorder

The page is now the cross-age diagnostic owner and is explicitly separated from applied child/school resources.

Key corrections:

- normal developmental separation anxiety is separated from developmentally excessive, persistent and impairing disorder-level anxiety;
- both childhood and adult presentations are covered;
- school refusal is treated as a behavior with multiple possible causes, not a synonym for SAD;
- physical symptoms can accompany anxiety but are not automatically assumed psychological;
- social anxiety, GAD, panic/agoraphobia, PTSD, developmental needs, depression, bullying and learning difficulty are part of differential interpretation;
- family accommodation is addressed without blaming parents;
- CBT and graded exposure are presented as structured, measured treatment rather than forced flooding;
- parent work and school return planning focus on restoring age-appropriate function;
- AACAP pediatric anxiety medication evidence is correctly framed as broader pediatric-anxiety evidence, not a separation-specific self-treatment claim;
- the 2026 childhood scoping review is presented with its small and heterogeneous evidence base;
- adult SAD is explicitly recognized while the lack of robust adult-specific treatment trials is stated;
- outcomes include attendance, independent sleep/activity, reassurance reduction and completion of graded separation steps;
- reassessment reopens medical, school, bullying, learning and other psychiatric explanations.

Intent-preserving internal architecture:

- `/encyclopedia/separation-anxiety-disorder/` — diagnostic owner across ages;
- `/terms/separation-anxiety/` — normal vs pathological separation-anxiety concept;
- `/content/separation-anxiety-child` — practical child/family support;
- `/care-guides/separation-anxiety-school-transition/` — applied school-transition guide;
- `/sectors/child/separation-anxiety/` — child-sector applied resource.

Evidence anchors: WHO ICD-11 CDDR (2024), AACAP anxiety guideline, the 2024 CBT research review/case study, the 2026 childhood separation-anxiety scoping review, and the 2026 review/commentary on the under-recognized adult disorder and its treatment-evidence gap.

## Duplicate-intent / canonical policy

The duplicate check is intent-based, not a string-match deletion exercise.

Pages that mention a Wave 006 disorder are retained when they serve a different function, for example:

- BDD evidence guide versus BDD diagnostic reference;
- trichotillomania practical tracking/help-seeking guide versus diagnostic reference;
- separation-anxiety glossary, child/family and school-transition resources versus cross-age diagnostic reference;
- related somatic-symptom, depersonalization/derealization and body-image pages that are distinct entities or intents.

No Wave 006 canonical or slug was changed and no published page was deleted.

## Safety rules preserved

- No self-diagnosis shortcut is introduced.
- No medication dose, self-directed switch, taper or supplement regimen is introduced.
- Medical/neurological warning signs take priority when applicable.
- BDD self-harm risk is explicitly escalated to urgent support when present.
- Dissociative-amnesia content does not endorse suggestive memory retrieval or claim historical truth from recovered-memory vividness.
- Separation-anxiety exposure is graded and planned, not coercive flooding.
- Trichotillomania content includes trichophagia and tissue-injury safety without punitive restraint.
- No external clinical review or partner approval is claimed.
- Existing review timestamps and reviewer identity are preserved rather than fabricated.
