# Rawafid Family Reading & Wellbeing Companion — flagship brief

Status: PREPARATION ONLY — not approved by Worldreader, not for public release yet.

Working Arabic title: **نقرأ معًا، ننمو معًا — دليل الأسرة للقراءة والصحة النفسية والأمان والانتماء**

Working English title: **Read Together, Grow Together — A Family Guide to Reading, Emotional Wellbeing, Safety & Belonging**

## 1. Purpose

Build one high-quality source that can be published in three synchronized formats:

1. Arabic web pages on Rawafid / Health Renewal.
2. An accessible Arabic PDF booklet.
3. An accessible English PDF booklet.

The guide is designed as an optional companion to family reading, not as a replacement for BookSmart, a child-protection program, mental-health care, pediatric care, or individualized professional advice.

If Worldreader welcomes the concept, a campaign edition may be adapted to the All Families Reading Challenge (AFRC) with their approved wording, links, logo treatment and brand rules. Until then, the editorial core remains a Rawafid working draft and must not imply Worldreader review, endorsement or authorship.

## 2. Strategic value

The companion should add something distinct and measurable to the campaign: it helps caregivers turn reading time into responsive conversation, emotional connection, language practice, body-safety discussion, social learning and family belonging without burdening the family with long lessons.

It should make Rawafid useful in a way that can be demonstrated after the challenge: families can arrive for BookSmart, use the reading companion, return for practical guidance, and continue using the resource after September.

## 3. Core editorial promise

Every section must answer four questions:

- **What is the child learning or experiencing?**
- **What can the caregiver do today?**
- **What can the caregiver say, in simple words?**
- **What should the caregiver avoid because it may confuse, shame, frighten or silence the child?**

No chapter may exist only to explain theory. Science must end in a usable action, phrase, prompt or routine.

## 4. Audience

Primary audience: caregivers of young children.

Campaign-specific age wording remains pending Worldreader confirmation because their current materials are not fully consistent. The Worldreader brand guide strongly centers children ages 0–5, Julia's email described the selected challenge books as particularly geared to ages 3–5, while other campaign-facing copy appears broader. ## 5. Content architecture

### Part I — Reading as a relationship

1. **Reading is not a test** — build a pleasant, predictable routine.
2. **Notice, name, wait, expand, connect** — a simple responsive-reading method.
3. **Let the child lead sometimes** — follow interest, point, gesture, repeat and revisit.

### Part II — Emotional wellbeing and resilience

4. **A child can feel difficult feelings and still be safe** — validation and co-regulation.
5. **Resilience is not emotional hardness** — supportive relationships, recovery and small opportunities for mastery.
6. **Repair after conflict** — a caregiver can set a limit, make a mistake, apologize and reconnect.

### Part III — Self-worth, comparison, contentment and gratitude

7. **My value is not a ranking** — reduce sibling, school, appearance and possession comparisons.
8. **Contentment without killing ambition** — appreciate what exists while still learning and trying.
9. **Gratitude without forced happiness** — notice good things without using gratitude to dismiss sadness, anger or disappointment.

### Part IV — Body safety and boundaries

10. **My body deserves respect** — accurate body-part language, privacy and bodily autonomy in developmentally appropriate language.
11. **The rule applies to familiar people too** — avoid an incomplete “stranger danger” model.
12. **Safe, necessary, unwanted and confusing touch** — context, explanation, consent where applicable, care/medical exceptions, and the child's right to speak up.
13. **No child carries the responsibility for preventing abuse** — adults are responsible for safety.
14. **Tell, and keep telling** — identify trusted adults and reinforce that the child will not be blamed for reporting discomfort or harm.

### Part V — Social confidence without people-pleasing

15. **Joining play and conversation** — simple social-entry skills.
16. **Kindness is not submission** — respect others while saying no, stopping, asking for space or seeking help.
17. **Confidence without aggression** — voice, posture, words and repair.

### Part VI — Family belonging, respect and autonomy

18. **Belonging: I have a place in this family** — routines, stories, shared time and predictable affection.
19. **Respect goes both ways** — parents guide and set limits; the child is still treated with dignity.
20. **Attachment without helpless dependence** — caregiver as secure base, with age-appropriate choice and independence.
21. **Disagreement is not betrayal** — teach respectful disagreement and emotional safety.

### Part VII — Inclusive family reading

22. **Communication is bigger than speech** — accept pointing, gesture, pictures, AAC or short responses.
23. **Do not force eye contact or performance** — engagement can look different across children.
24. **Reduce sensory load** — shorter sessions, quieter spaces, repetition and predictable routines when helpful.
25. **A child may enjoy the same book repeatedly** — repetition can support participation and word learning.

### Part VIII — BookSmart / digital shared reading (campaign edition only if approved)

26. **The adult remains part of the reading** — the device should support, not replace, interaction.
27. **Worldreader image rules must be followed** — family-reading situations; no child reading alone with a phone; child should not hold the phone; no tablets; no horizontal-phone reading image; ages 0–5 strongly preferred in campaign imagery; releases required for family/child images.
28. **BookSmart and Worldreader spelling must remain exact** — never “Booksmart” or “World Reader.”

## 6. Chapter micro-format

Every public chapter uses the same low-friction sequence:

1. **A real moment** — one recognizable family situation.
2. **What may be happening?** — 2–4 short evidence-aligned points.
3. **Try this now** — 3–5 actions.
4. **Words you can use** — 2–4 natural caregiver phrases.
5. **Avoid this trap** — one common but counterproductive response.
6. **Use it while reading** — 1–3 prompts that work with many stories.
7. **Adapt it** — age / communication / sensory adaptations where needed.
8. **When to seek more help** — only when clinically or safeguarding relevant.
9. **Sources and evidence note** — concise and traceable.

## 7. Evidence discipline

Use an explicit claim-strength system in internal review:

- **A — guideline / strong synthesis:** official guideline, policy statement, systematic review or meta-analysis directly supports the practical claim.
- **B — converging evidence:** multiple studies or a good review support the concept, but the exact family practice has less direct trial evidence.
- **C — cautious practice suggestion:** low-risk, developmentally coherent practice offered without claiming clinical benefit.
- **Do not publish as fact:** claim is promotional, causal or clinical beyond the evidence.

Examples:

- Shared reading supports language development: **A**.
- Responsive caregiver-child interaction supports early development: **A**.
- Sensitive caregiving is associated with attachment security: **A/B**, depending on exact wording.
- Parental autonomy support is associated with better child wellbeing across cultures: **A for association/meta-analysis; do not turn it into a guaranteed individual outcome**.
- Gratitude prevents child mental illness: **Do not publish**.
- Gratitude can be used as a short family reflection practice while allowing difficult feelings: **C/B depending on wording and age**.
- Social media comparison causes poor self-esteem in every child: **Do not publish**; evidence is heterogeneous and largely older youth/adults.

## 8. Source hierarchy

Priority order:

1. WHO / UNICEF / other global normative guidance.
2. American Academy of Pediatrics and equivalent professional guidance.
3. High-quality systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
4. Strong primary studies only when needed to answer a gap.
5. Reputable implementation resources for plain-language application, clearly distinguished from evidence claims.

## 9. PDF-ready authoring rules

The Arabic and English editions must be mirrors in structure and meaning, not literal sentence-by-sentence translations.

- Single canonical content map and stable chapter IDs.
- No web-only phrases such as “click here” in canonical copy.
- Links stored separately so PDF can use short labels and QR codes.
- Short paragraphs; avoid dense walls of text.
- Recurring callout types: **جرّب / Try**, **قل / Say**, **تجنب / Avoid**, **تذكر / Remember**, **اطلب دعمًا / Seek support**.
- Images and decorative elements never carry essential meaning alone.
- All meaningful illustrations receive alt text in web edition and tagged alternatives in accessible PDF.
- Tables are minimized; family guidance should read naturally on mobile and in print.
- References use stable DOI / official URLs and a compact endnote system.

## 10. Visual direction

The desired feeling is: calm, capable, warm, modern, spacious and trustworthy — not childish clutter and not a clinical brochure.

- Generous white space.
- One idea per visual block.
- Soft geometric forms that help reading order rather than decorate empty space.
- Illustrations show interaction, not passive posing.
- Body-safety pages use calm, non-frightening visual language.
- Never use shame, fear or graphic imagery to teach safety.
- Accessibility contrast is non-negotiable.

If Worldreader co-branding is approved, follow their supplied guide: Montserrat for their corporate English brand treatment; approved logos only and never altered; complementary corporate colors; family-reading imagery; the device and child-positioning rules listed above. Arabic typography requires explicit compatibility review because their supplied guide does not define an Arabic corporate typeface.
