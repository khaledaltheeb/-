# Specialist Knowledge Centers — Architecture and Source Governance

Date: 2026-09-11

## Purpose

This document defines content ownership, source roles, terminology and anti-duplication rules for two specialist knowledge centers on Health Renewal / Rawafid.

## 1. Acquired Brain Injury center

### Taxonomy

- Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`
- Parent section: `neurological-rehabilitation`
- Specialist section: `acquired-brain-injury-recovery`
- Editorial hub: `legacy-outside-box-acquired-brain-injury`

### Content ownership

The TBI and stroke rehabilitation guides remain condition-level clinical/functional overviews. The ABI specialist pages own narrower cross-condition intents:

- PTA owns early post-traumatic amnesia questions.
- Attention owns sustained/selective/divided attention and functional attentional load.
- Processing speed owns latency, speed–accuracy trade-off and time-sensitive accommodations.
- Memory owns new learning, prospective memory and external memory systems.
- Executive/self-awareness owns planning, monitoring, inhibition, goal management and awareness.
- Cognitive-communication owns discourse/pragmatics/contextual communication when basic language may be relatively preserved.
- Fatigue owns pacing and energy management.
- Sleep owns sleep assessment and its interaction with fatigue/cognition.
- Emotional/behavioural change owns affect regulation and behaviour after ABI.
- Mental health owns depression, anxiety, trauma and specialist mental-health pathways.
- Family/caregiver owns caregiver capacity, training, burden, handover and sustainability.
- Return-to-education owns school/university re-entry and academic accommodations.
- Return-to-work owns vocational rehabilitation, work trials, graded return and retention.
- Identity/adjustment owns sense of self, role reconstruction, grief and meaning.
- Relationships/intimacy owns relationship roles, sexuality, consent, privacy and partner support.
- Driving/community mobility owns fitness-to-drive assessment, licensing boundaries and alternatives.

If a proposed page overlaps one of these owners, enrich the owner rather than create a new canonical.

## 2. Inclusive Education Practice, Leadership and Systems center

### Taxonomy

- Sector: `special-needs-inclusion`
- Parent section: `inclusion-accommodations`
- Specialist section: `inclusive-education-practice-systems`
- Editorial hub: `legacy-learning-path-evidence-guided-inclusive-education-foundations`

### Content ownership

- Inclusive leadership owns governance, barrier mapping, accountability and schoolwide implementation cycles.
- Family engagement owns system-level family partnership and shared decision processes.
- Inclusive academic practice owns schoolwide instructional design, scaffolding and evidence-based teaching.
- Social-emotional-behavioural systems owns PBIS/MTSS-style preventive and multi-tier supports, FBA boundaries and exclusion reduction.
- Assessment governance owns construct definition, accommodation governance, accessible assessment and review/appeal processes.
- Multidisciplinary collaboration owns role clarity, meetings, decision records and handoffs.
- Accessibility/assistive technology owns lifecycle governance from need and trial through procurement, configuration, training, maintenance and backup.
- Implementation fidelity owns whether the inclusive system is actually being implemented as intended.
- Quality indicators owns what outcomes and process indicators the school should monitor.
- Teacher professional learning owns sustained professional learning, coaching and transfer to practice.
- Data/monitoring owns data dictionary, EMIS, validation, lineage, versioning, privacy, disaggregation and continuous-improvement loops.
- Culturally/linguistically responsive inclusion owns language, identity, interpreter use, assessment bias, multilingual learners, refugee context and rights boundaries.

Existing glossary or practical pages remain valid and should link to these system-level owners rather than be replaced.

## 3. Source-role hierarchy

Sources are not interchangeable. Each source is assigned a role.

### Rights / normative layer
- UN CRPD, especially Article 24.

### International public-health / rehabilitation layer
- WHO rehabilitation materials.

### Clinical and rehabilitation guideline layer
- INCOG
- CAN-TBI
- Action Collaborative on TBI Care
- Canadian Stroke Best Practices
- KITE / evidence-linked rehabilitation guidance
- SCIRE for spinal cord injury evidence

### Patient/family information layer
- Headway
- MSKTC
- Stroke Foundation Arabic resources

Patient/family sources are valuable for lived-experience pathways and plain-language framing but should not be treated as the sole authority for high-stakes clinical prescriptions.

### Inclusive-education implementation layer
- CEC / CEEDAR High-Leverage Practices
- DISES professional learning
- CAST UDL 3.0
- Center on PBIS

### Data and monitoring layer
- UNICEF / Washington Group Child Functioning Module
- Child Functioning Module — Teacher Version
- Inclusive Education Module
- UNESCO monitoring guidance

### Jordan policy-context layer
- Jordan National Framework for Inclusion and Diversity in Education
- Jordan Education Strategic Plan 2026–2030

Rawafid resources may be aligned with or informed by these official documents, but must not be described as Ministry of Education instruments unless formally adopted.

## 4. Localization rules

### Stroke Foundation Australia
Use Arabic health information as a source, but do not transplant Australian phone numbers, services, referral pathways or regulatory rules into Jordan/Arab-region guidance.

### Arabic SCI physical-activity guidance
Use as a regional Arabic terminology/context source. Do not use it as the sole authority for individualized exercise prescription or dosage. Triangulate with current specialized SCI evidence and individualized professional assessment.

### Driving
Never copy waiting periods, seizure rules or medical licensing thresholds from the UK, US, Australia or another jurisdiction into Jordanian guidance. Use Jordanian official licensing sources for legal requirements.

### Professional titles
Scientific Arabic terminology is standardized in Rawafid, while legal/professional registration titles remain jurisdiction-dependent.

## 5. Copyright and translation rules

- Do not translate or republish protected Headway or other copyrighted source text verbatim without permission.
- Use sources to discover topics and evidence, then author an original Arabic synthesis.
- Translation of an assessment or scale does not establish psychometric validity.
- Respect test-security restrictions; do not publish protected standardized test items.

## 6. Terminology core

- Psychologist → `اختصاصي علم النفس`
- Clinical Psychologist → `اختصاصي علم النفس السريري`
- Neuropsychologist → `اختصاصي علم النفس العصبي`
- Psychiatrist → `طبيب اختصاصي في الطب النفسي`
- Physiatrist → `طبيب اختصاصي في الطب الفيزيائي وإعادة التأهيل`
- Acquired Brain Injury → `إصابة الدماغ المكتسبة`
- Traumatic Brain Injury → `إصابة الدماغ الرضّية`
- Stroke → `السكتة الدماغية`

Avoid translating `Psychologist` as `طبيب نفسي`.

## 7. Publication decision rule

For every proposed page:

1. identify the exact search intent;
2. search the live published corpus;
3. identify the current canonical owner, if any;
4. compare title/body intent and long-paragraph reuse;
5. decide `new`, `enrich`, or `merge`;
6. use authoritative/current sources appropriate to the claim type;
7. author original Arabic synthesis;
8. pass V6 release and originality gates;
9. publish indexable with canonical ownership intact;
10. add related-content graph edges to hub, parent guide and adjacent specialist pages.

Do not create a replacement page merely to meet a numeric publishing target.

## 8. Existing-page enrichment rule

Existing published pages must not be shortened, deleted or unpublished during enrichment. Use versioned zero-downtime updates, verify a previous snapshot exists, preserve canonical and taxonomy ownership, and add material only when it improves the page's intent or connects it to a deeper specialist owner.
