## Reuse and canonical policy
- Existing strong pages must be cross-listed rather than cloned.
- Existing canonical URLs are preserved when pregnancy, older-adult, adolescent, peer-recovery, treatment, withdrawal, harm-reduction or other pages are reused in the professional-learning journey.
- A new page is justified only when it performs a distinct professional-learning, clinical-decision, implementation or governance function not already served by an existing page.
- The research magazine remains a living-evidence layer, not a substitute for competency or guideline pages.
- The Addiction Atlas remains a substance/evidence product, not a professional curriculum.
- Cross-list counts may overlap; program size is always reported by unique `content.id` / canonical resources, not by summing taxonomy memberships.

## Safety and clinical boundaries
- Educational content must not diagnose an individual or replace a licensed clinician.
- No self-managed dangerous-withdrawal schedules, non-medical dosing instructions, mixing recipes, intoxication optimization or unsupported potency claims.
- Screening tools must not be described as stand-alone diagnoses.
- Professional-role pages must separate education from jurisdiction-specific scope of practice.
- US-specific regulation, DEA/FDA rules, reimbursement rules or program structures must be explicitly labeled and never generalized to Jordan or the wider Arab region.
- Medication availability, legality and referral pathways require local verification before regional claims are published.
- Pregnancy, adolescents, older adults, violence/coercion and other higher-risk contexts require population-appropriate safety framing and current specialist guidance.

## Evidence semantics
- A competency framework defines what professionals should know or be able to do; it is not automatically a current clinical-practice guideline.
- AMERSA 2018/2019 competency material may structure the curriculum while current guidance updates clinical recommendations.
- Research articles, systematic reviews, toolkits, position statements, consensus standards and clinical guidelines must remain distinguishable evidence types.
- Newer evidence does not automatically overrule an older authoritative guideline; update only when the evidence or governing source materially changes the claim.
- Research-magazine articles may inform a living-evidence layer but must not silently become clinical recommendations.

## Update triggers
Review a resource when one or more of the following occurs:
- a new or revised authoritative guideline or consensus statement changes a relevant recommendation;
- a major systematic review materially changes certainty, effect size or applicability;
- a safety, medication, regulatory or availability claim changes;
- implementation evidence exposes a workflow failure or local-adaptation issue;
- a canonical/duplicate audit identifies overlap;
- a route, sitemap, indexability or render-parity check fails;
- a partner supplies a newer framework/toolkit that materially changes the learning function.

Do not churn stable content merely because a new paper exists. Update the affected claim and preserve unaffected high-quality material.

## Communication rule
No external implementation-update email should be sent merely because taxonomy or drafts exist. For AMERSA or another source partner, correspondence should follow a verified public release and should state exactly what was implemented, attribute sources correctly, avoid endorsement claims, and ask concrete review/permission/technical-assistance questions only after the published work has been independently checked.
