## 4. Rawafid’s added value — evidence-based family guidance

Rawafid should not merely repost Worldreader’s campaign. Its differentiating contribution should be a short, practical Arabic guide that helps caregivers make shared reading interactive, responsive and inclusive.

### Evidence basis

The guidance should be framed conservatively around the following established evidence:

- The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends shared reading beginning in infancy and continuing through at least kindergarten, emphasizing reciprocal, interactive reading and caregiver–child engagement.
- A systematic review/meta-analysis of shared picture-book interventions in children roughly 1–6 years found positive effects on expressive and receptive language and a strong improvement in caregiver book-sharing competence.
- WHO’s Nurturing Care framework emphasizes responsive caregiving: noticing, understanding and appropriately responding to the child’s signals while creating enjoyable early-learning opportunities in everyday relationships.

**No page should promise that a reading challenge will diagnose, treat or prevent developmental/mental health conditions.** Benefits should be described as supporting language, early literacy, interaction, shared attention and family routines — not as guaranteed outcomes for any individual child.

## 6. Family-reading micro-guide: approved content principles

### Do

- Follow the child’s attention.
- Ask simple, genuine questions rather than testing memory continuously.
- Accept non-verbal responses.
- Expand the child’s response by one idea.
- Re-read favorites.
- Stop or shorten when the interaction becomes stressful.
- Praise engagement and curiosity, not only correct answers.
- Make the routine predictable and low-friction.

### Avoid

- Turning every page into a quiz.
- Correcting every pronunciation error.
- Forcing eye contact or spoken answers.
- Comparing siblings’ reading performance.
- Promising developmental gains from a fixed number of books.
- Presenting screen time itself as the mechanism of benefit; the target is shared, responsive interaction around the story.
