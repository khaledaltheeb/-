# Rehabilitation clinical gap map — wave 4

Date: 2026-09-06
Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`

## Selection rule

Wave 4 was selected only after checking the production taxonomy for durable pathway gaps. The target topics had no equivalent published reference guide in the rehabilitation sector and could be supported by current specialty and guideline sources without reproducing protected tools.

## Gaps closed

### Amputation and limb loss
The musculoskeletal category contained procedure/study material but lacked an end-to-end rehabilitation pathway after limb loss.

New pathway: `amputation-limb-loss-rehabilitation-guide`

Coverage includes:
- perioperative rehabilitation;
- residual-limb care and skin;
- edema/shaping and contracture prevention;
- pain and phantom sensations;
- strength, balance, transfers and wheelchair mobility;
- prosthetic readiness and fitting;
- gait training and energy cost;
- upper-limb and lower-limb functional differences;
- work, driving/community mobility and participation;
- device maintenance and long-term follow-up.

### Parkinson disease
The neurological category had no durable Parkinson rehabilitation guide.

New pathway: `parkinson-disease-rehabilitation-guide`

Coverage includes:
- exercise and physical activity;
- gait, freezing, cueing and turning;
- balance and fall prevention;
- strength and flexibility;
- occupational therapy and daily routines;
- voice, communication and swallowing;
- cognition and dual-task demands;
- home/environmental adaptation;
- work and community participation;
- symptom and medication timing as a rehabilitation-planning factor.

### Post-ICU / critical illness
The cardiopulmonary category lacked a recovery pathway for people leaving intensive care after critical illness.

New pathway: `post-icu-critical-illness-rehabilitation-guide`

Coverage includes:
- ICU-acquired weakness and deconditioning;
- mobility and endurance;
- respiratory recovery;
- swallowing/nutrition;
- cognition, delirium-related consequences and attention;
- anxiety, depression, PTSD and sleep;
- family/caregiver burden;
- discharge continuity;
- return to work/education/community;
- when to reopen rehabilitation after discharge.

### Cancer rehabilitation
The dedicated cancer-rehabilitation category lacked a durable umbrella pathway across the oncology continuum.

New pathway: `cancer-rehabilitation-guide`

Coverage includes:
- prehabilitation;
- rehabilitation during active treatment;
- fatigue, weakness, pain and mobility;
- peripheral neuropathy and fall risk;
- lymphedema and postoperative limitations;
- cognition and work participation;
- survivorship;
- supportive/palliative rehabilitation;
- exercise-safety decisions and oncology-specific precautions;
- outcome measurement without copying protected instruments.

## Evidence quality boundaries

- AAPM&R / PM&R KnowledgeNow is used as a specialty anchor where available.
- NICE, VA/DoD, ASCO, WHO and other authoritative guidance are used according to the clinical pathway.
- Cancer exercise content avoids one-size-fits-all frequency/intensity/duration prescriptions; ASCO supports aerobic/resistance exercise during appropriate active treatment but notes insufficient evidence for a single universal prescription.
- Post-ICU content is multidomain and not limited to physical weakness.
- Prosthetic and assistive-device content follows a service pathway, not product marketing.
- No protected scoring sheets or proprietary assessments are republished.
