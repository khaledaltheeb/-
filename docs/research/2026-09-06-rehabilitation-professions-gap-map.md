# Rehabilitation professions gap map — wave 2

Date: 2026-09-06
Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`
Category: `rehabilitation-professions`
Production: https://healthrenewal.org

## Baseline

Before this wave, the category contained one published/indexable page only: `multidisciplinary-rehabilitation-team`. That page explains teamwork as a system, but it did not answer role-specific questions such as when to refer to each profession, what each profession assesses, how responsibilities overlap, or how role-specific results are translated into shared functional goals.

## Editorial decision

Do not duplicate the generic multidisciplinary-team page. Build durable role guides with five common rules:

1. Explain the profession's distinct contribution to rehabilitation.
2. Start from a functional question rather than a title or procedure.
3. Explain overlap with other professions and where duplication becomes waste.
4. Avoid exporting a US/UK scope-of-practice rule into Jordan or another jurisdiction; legal scope and licensure must be verified locally.
5. Link to authoritative professional/WHO sources without reproducing protected tests, competency tools, scoring sheets, or proprietary materials.

## Published role library

### `physiatrist-role-rehabilitation-guide`
Explains Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation / physiatry as the medical-functional layer: diagnosis, medical complications, spasticity/pain and procedures where appropriate, equipment prescriptions, multidisciplinary planning, transitions, and long-term function.

Evidence families: AAPM&R; WHO rehabilitation workforce; WHO Rehabilitation Competency Framework; WHO ICF.

### `physical-therapy-role-rehabilitation-guide`
Covers movement-system assessment, strength, balance, gait, transfers, aerobic capacity, pain, neurological and cardiopulmonary rehabilitation, home programs, devices, community mobility, and outcome measurement.

Evidence families: APTA Guide to Physical Therapist Practice; APTA scope resources; World Physiotherapy; WHO RCF/ICF.

### `occupational-therapy-role-rehabilitation-guide`
Covers activities of daily living, hand and upper-limb function, functional cognition, routines, energy management, home/environmental modification, assistive technology, study/work, caregiver training, community mobility and participation.

Evidence families: AOTA domain/process and practice resources; WFOT; WHO RCF/ICF.

### `speech-language-pathology-role-rehabilitation-guide`
Separates speech, language, aphasia, motor speech, voice, cognitive-communication, AAC, multilingual assessment, swallowing/dysphagia, partner training and participation outcomes.

Evidence families: ASHA Scope of Practice and Practice Portal resources; WHO RCF/ICF.

### `rehabilitation-nursing-role-guide`
Explains 24-hour rehabilitation nursing: functional monitoring, skin/pressure management, bladder and bowel routines, medication effects, pain/sleep, safe mobility between therapy sessions, swallowing/nutrition plan implementation, self-management education, caregiver training and discharge transition.

Evidence families: Association of Rehabilitation Nurses; American Nurses Association; WHO workforce/RCF/ICF.

### `rehabilitation-psychology-neuropsychology-role-guide`
Distinguishes rehabilitation psychology from clinical neuropsychology. Covers adjustment, identity, mood, anxiety, chronic pain, behavior after brain injury, cognitive assessment, actionable memory/executive strategies, caregiver/family issues, study/work, capacity-related questions, cultural/linguistic limits and test security.

Evidence families: APA Division 22; ABPP Rehabilitation Psychology; American Academy of Clinical Neuropsychology; WHO workforce/RCF/ICF.

### `rehabilitation-social-work-case-management-role-guide`
Separates health social work from case management. Covers psychosocial and environmental assessment, rights/resources, caregiver capacity, housing/transport, discharge planning, work/study, safeguarding, service coordination and transitions.

Evidence families: NASW health-care and case-management standards; Case Management Society of America; WHO workforce/RCF/ICF.

### `prosthetics-orthotics-seating-assistive-technology-role-guide`
Covers prosthetics, orthotics, wheelchair/seating services and assistive technology as a service pathway: goal and environment assessment, trial, fitting, training, pressure/skin management, digital access, maintenance, repair, abandonment risk, funding/access and team responsibilities.

Evidence families: WHO Standards for Prosthetics and Orthotics; WHO Wheelchair Provision Guidelines 2023; WHO Assistive Technology; RESNA ATP and service guidance; WHO RCF/ICF.

## Cross-role model

The category now supports a clear referral model:

- **Medical-functional integration:** physiatry / PM&R physician.
- **Movement, mobility and physical capacity:** physical therapy.
- **Daily activities, roles, environment and functional cognition:** occupational therapy.
- **Speech, language, cognitive-communication, AAC and swallowing:** speech-language pathology.
- **Continuous clinical-functional implementation and self-management:** rehabilitation nursing.
- **Adjustment, behavior, mood and specialized brain-behavior assessment:** rehabilitation psychology / neuropsychology.
- **Psychosocial barriers, resources, rights and service continuity:** social work / case management.
- **Device-service lifecycle:** prosthetics, orthotics, seating, wheelchair and assistive-technology specialists.

These are not rigid global legal boundaries. Overlap is expected and local regulation governs protected acts and professional scope.

## Rights and attribution boundary

- Do not publish full psychological/neuropsychological tests, proprietary scoring sheets, protected OT/PT/SLP assessment tools, or certification exam materials.
- Do not imply endorsement, accreditation, certification or review by AAPM&R, APTA, AOTA, WFOT, ASHA, ARN, APA, ABPP, AACN, NASW, CMSA, RESNA or WHO merely because they are cited.
- Use organization names to identify evidence provenance only.
- When local scope, licensure, benefits or legal rights are discussed, use official local sources and record the verification date.
