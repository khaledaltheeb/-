# Rehabilitation expansion gap map — AAPM&R-led wave 1

Date: 2026-09-06
Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`
Production: https://healthrenewal.org

## Objective

Expand the Arabic rehabilitation sector only where there is a real editorial gap, using AAPM&R / PM&R KnowledgeNow as a specialty anchor and pairing it with current primary guidelines, systematic reviews, standards, and WHO functioning/rehabilitation frameworks. The work must not translate or republish proprietary patient handouts or measurement instruments; it creates original Arabic evidence syntheses and links readers to the authoritative source.

## Production baseline before wave 1

The production database contained 18 active rehabilitation categories and 74 unique published/indexable pages. Category-level review showed that family rehabilitation, assistive technology, sensory rehabilitation, and community/vocational rehabilitation were already comparatively strong, while several high-value clinical pathways were represented mainly by isolated magazine/research-analysis pages rather than durable reference guides.

Key baseline gaps:

| Category | Published pages before wave 1 | Gap observed |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Neurological rehabilitation | 7 | Research analyses existed, but no durable end-to-end guides for stroke, traumatic brain injury, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, or spasticity management. |
| Cardiopulmonary rehabilitation | 2 | No comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation pathway and no full COPD pulmonary-rehabilitation pathway. |
| Musculoskeletal rehabilitation | 6 | Mostly study-specific pages; no durable knee osteoarthritis rehabilitation guide. |
| Rehabilitation professions | 1 | Major structural gap: the multidisciplinary team is not yet explained role-by-role. |
| Rehabilitation in Jordan and MENA | 0 | Complete local/regional evidence and access gap. Requires an official-source wave rather than generic international copy. |

## Wave 1 selection logic

Topics were selected when they met all of the following:

1. A clear production gap existed after checking the live taxonomy and published pages.
2. AAPM&R or PM&R KnowledgeNow provided a credible specialty anchor.
3. Current external guidance could be triangulated with specialty sources rather than relying on one organization.
4. The topic supported a durable clinical/functioning pathway, not a duplicate news or study-summary page.
5. The page could be written without reproducing protected instruments, worksheets, or proprietary handouts.

## Released wave 1

### Neurological rehabilitation

- `stroke-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R CNS rehabilitation.
  - Primary guidance: Canadian Stroke Best Practices 7th Edition 2025; NICE NG236; WHO rehabilitation/ICF.
  - Gap closed: complete pathway from early assessment through mobility, upper limb, swallowing, communication, cognition, family, discharge, participation, and outcome measurement.

- `traumatic-brain-injury-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R traumatic brain injury.
  - Primary guidance: VA/DoD post-acute mild TBI; INCOG 2.0 cognitive rehabilitation; WHO ICF.
  - Gap closed: consciousness/post-traumatic amnesia, cognition, behavior, communication, mobility, family, study/work, driving, and long-term participation.

- `spinal-cord-injury-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R traumatic SCI rehabilitation.
  - Primary guidance: Consortium for Spinal Cord Medicine / PVA; SCIRE; ASIA ISNCSCI 9th Edition 2026; WHO.
  - Gap closed: neurological classification, respiratory/autonomic issues, skin, bowel/bladder, seating and wheeled mobility, pain/spasticity, sexuality, work, equipment, aging, and lifelong follow-up.
  - Rights boundary: ISNCSCI is explained and linked to the official ASIA resources; the worksheet is not republished.

- `multiple-sclerosis-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R multiple sclerosis.
  - Primary guidance: NICE NG220 reviewed 2026-06-03; Cochrane; current exercise/fatigue evidence; WHO.
  - Gap closed: fatigue, exercise, mobility, balance, spasticity, cognition, self-care, work, environmental modification, relapse recovery, and long-term self-management.

- `spasticity-rehabilitation-management-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R Clinical Guidance on Spasticity 2024 and KnowledgeNow.
  - Primary guidance: Canadian Stroke Best Practices 2025; NICE; SCIRE; WHO ICF.
  - Gap closed: functional assessment, triggers, goals, rehabilitation, oral/focal/advanced options, contracture differentiation, family burden, and outcome measurement across neurological diagnoses.

### Cardiopulmonary rehabilitation

- `cardiac-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R cardiac rehabilitation.
  - Primary guidance: AHA/AACVPR Core Components 2024; AHA home-based cardiac rehabilitation; WHO.
  - Gap closed: referral, phases, risk/functional assessment, aerobic and resistance training, secondary prevention, nutrition, medication routines, psychosocial care, multimorbidity, hybrid delivery, return to roles, and maintenance.

- `copd-pulmonary-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R COPD pulmonary rehabilitation, updated 2025-12-05.
  - Primary guidance: official ATS pulmonary rehabilitation guideline; ATS implementation resources; GOLD 2026; WHO.
  - Gap closed: exercise, strength, dyspnea and energy management, exacerbation recovery, inhaler/oxygen logistics, nutrition, psychosocial care, home/hybrid rehabilitation, environment/travel, multimorbidity, and maintenance.

### Musculoskeletal rehabilitation

- `knee-osteoarthritis-rehabilitation-guide`
  - Specialty anchor: AAPM&R Knee Osteoarthritis, updated 2026-05-07.
  - Primary guidance: NICE NG226; ACR/Arthritis Foundation; OARSI; WHO ICF.
  - Gap closed: functional diagnosis, exercise dose, strength, aerobic activity, weight when relevant, flare management, devices, sleep/fear, work, pre-operative preparation, post-arthroplasty rehabilitation, load progression, and escalation to surgical assessment.

- Musculoskeletal rehabilitation: **7** pages (from 6).

## Quality contract applied

Wave-1 editorial pages satisfy the current V6 release contract, including:

- at least 2,500 Arabic-token words;
- at least 8 H2 and 4 H3 headings;
- at least 6 FAQs;
- at least 8 explicit search-intent questions;
- at least 5 references with multiple primary/guideline/systematic-review sources;
- at least 5 claim-to-source mappings;
- taxonomy review, classification rationale, originality record, evidence-led rewrite declaration, and page mechanism metadata;
- centralized disclaimer route rather than duplicated inline warning/disclaimer copy.

## Rights and attribution boundary

- Do not copy or translate AAPM&R patient handouts verbatim without explicit permission.
- Do not republish full protected measurement instruments, worksheets, or scoring forms merely because they are clinically common.
- Prefer original Arabic explanation + official-source link + rights note when a tool has reuse restrictions.
- Organization names in references indicate evidence provenance only; they do not imply endorsement, partnership, certification, or review of Rawafid.

## Priority wave 2

### A. Rehabilitation professions — highest structural gap

Current coverage: 1 page.

Recommended durable role guides:

1. Physiatry / Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation physician.
2. Physical therapy in rehabilitation.
3. Occupational therapy in rehabilitation.
4. Speech-language pathology, communication, cognition, and swallowing.
5. Rehabilitation nursing.
6. Rehabilitation psychology / neuropsychology.
7. Social work, case management, and care coordination.
8. Prosthetics, orthotics, seating, and assistive-technology specialists.

Each role guide should explain scope, referral triggers, what the assessment adds, where roles overlap, where they differ, and how multidisciplinary goals are coordinated. Avoid professional-scope claims that vary by jurisdiction unless an official local source is cited.

### B. Jordan and MENA — zero-page gap

Do not fill this category with generic international material. Build a dedicated official-source wave using, at minimum where relevant and current:

- Jordan Ministry of Health;
- Higher Council for the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (Jordan);
- Jordanian laws/regulations and official service directories;
- WHO EMRO and WHO rehabilitation/system-strengthening resources;
- official national or regional workforce/service data;
- credible university/health-system or peer-reviewed data when official data are incomplete.

Candidate pages: access pathways, rights and reasonable accommodation, rehabilitation service map methodology, workforce and service gaps, community-based rehabilitation, assistive-device access, and continuity across hospital/community/education/work.

### C. Next disease/pathway gaps after A and B

- Parkinson disease rehabilitation.
- Low back pain rehabilitation.
- Hip osteoarthritis rehabilitation.
- Shoulder rehabilitation / rotator-cuff pathway.
- Rehabilitation before and after major joint replacement as a dedicated pathway.

## Decision

Wave 1 is complete in production. Next work should prioritize **rehabilitation professions** and **Jordan/MENA**, because those are now the largest structural gaps after the clinical reference expansion.
