# Rehabilitation clinical gap map — waves 4 and 5

Date: 2026-09-06
Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`

## Method

Topics were added only when all of the following were true:

1. live production did not contain an equivalent durable reference pathway;
2. the topic could be anchored in current specialty/guideline evidence;
3. the content would add a distinct user journey rather than restate an existing research summary;
4. the pathway could be written without redistributing protected instruments or proprietary forms;
5. the page could satisfy the production V6 depth, source and taxonomy contract.

## Wave 4 gaps closed

### Limb loss / amputation
Gap: no end-to-end rehabilitation pathway after limb loss.

Added: `amputation-limb-loss-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: perioperative rehabilitation, residual-limb care, pain/phantom symptoms, mobility, prosthetic readiness/fitting, gait, work and long-term follow-up.

### Parkinson disease
Gap: no durable Parkinson-specific rehabilitation guide.

Added: `parkinson-disease-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: exercise, gait/freezing/cueing, falls, occupational function, voice/swallowing, cognition, home adaptation and symptom/medication timing.

### Critical illness / post-ICU
Gap: no integrated recovery pathway after intensive care.

Added: `post-icu-critical-illness-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: ICU-acquired weakness, endurance, respiratory recovery, swallowing/nutrition, cognition, sleep, mental health, caregiver burden and return to work/community.

### Cancer rehabilitation
Gap: no umbrella pathway spanning the oncology continuum.

Added: `cancer-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: prehabilitation, active treatment, fatigue, pain, neuropathy, lymphedema, cognition, work, survivorship and supportive/palliative rehabilitation.

## Wave 5 gaps closed

### Low back pain and sciatica
Gap: no comprehensive rehabilitation pathway; only other musculoskeletal research pages existed.

Added: `low-back-pain-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: clinical triage, imaging decisions, activity, exercise, psychosocial factors, manual-therapy role, sciatica escalation, work return, recurrent-flare self-management.

### Rotator cuff and shoulder
Gap: the only shoulder-related production result was a study-summary page about telerehabilitation after shoulder arthroplasty, not a reference pathway for rotator-cuff disease.

Added: `rotator-cuff-shoulder-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: differential diagnosis, imaging context, non-operative rehabilitation, range and progressive strength, overhead work/sport, injection context, surgery and postoperative rehabilitation principles.

### Hip osteoarthritis
Gap: knee osteoarthritis had a durable guide, but hip osteoarthritis did not.

Added: `hip-osteoarthritis-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: gait, hip-spine interaction, range, chair rise, stairs, walking aids, exercise, work/travel, flare management, total-hip-arthroplasty referral and pre/post-operative rehabilitation.

### Heart-transplant rehabilitation
Gap: general cardiac rehabilitation existed, but the denervated-heart physiology, pre-transplant conditioning, immunosuppression and transplant-specific long-term recovery were not covered.

Added: `heart-transplant-rehabilitation-guide`

Distinct value: prehabilitation, early mobility, altered heart-rate response, exercise monitoring, strength, infection/immunosuppression issues, nutrition, bone health, psychosocial adjustment and return to life roles.

## Verified release state

All eight pages are published/indexable and linked to the intended category with 6–7 central source-registry records each.

After wave 5, `rehabilitation-functioning` contains **95 unique published/indexable pages**.

## Remaining AAPM&R-led opportunities

The strongest next specialty gaps, subject to a fresh production check before authoring, are:

- pulmonary rehabilitation before and after lung transplantation;
- liver and kidney transplant rehabilitation;
- additional medical-rehabilitation pathways not already represented in production;
- joint-replacement content only where a new page would provide a genuinely distinct pathway beyond the existing knee, hip and shoulder material.

## Editorial rule for future waves

Do not increase page count for its own sake. A new page must close a user-journey gap, have current authoritative evidence, and survive semantic comparison against the live production corpus before authoring.
