# Transplant rehabilitation gap map — wave 6

Date: 2026-09-06
Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`

## Gap selection

A live production search showed that the sector already had:
- a general cardiac-rehabilitation pathway;
- a COPD pulmonary-rehabilitation pathway;
- a heart-transplant rehabilitation pathway;
- adult/geriatric rehabilitation content.

It did **not** have durable pathways for:
- lung transplantation;
- liver and kidney transplantation.

These were selected because AAPM&R maintains explicit rehabilitation topics for them and current evidence allows clinically meaningful, non-duplicative Arabic pathways.

## Lung transplantation

New page: `lung-transplant-rehabilitation-guide`

Why it is not a duplicate of COPD:
- transplant candidacy and prehabilitation;
- oxygen logistics while awaiting transplant;
- frailty and sarcopenia in transplant candidates;
- postoperative/ICU recovery;
- immunosuppression and infection-aware activity;
- graft/rejection follow-up context;
- post-intubation swallowing/voice issues;
- long-term transplant-specific work, travel and community participation.

Current high-value evidence:
- AAPM&R pulmonary-transplant rehabilitation;
- ISHLT candidate-selection consensus (2021);
- **ISHLT 2026 frailty consensus** for lung-transplant candidates;
- ISHLT perioperative/ICU consensus;
- ATS pulmonary-rehabilitation guidance.

## Liver and kidney transplantation

New page: `liver-kidney-transplant-rehabilitation-guide`

Why the two organs share a page:
- both are abdominal solid-organ transplants with substantial overlap in frailty, sarcopenia, prehabilitation, abdominal-wound recovery, immunosuppression, bone/metabolic effects and return to life roles;
- the page preserves organ-specific sections for dialysis/kidney and cirrhosis/hepatic cognitive/nutritional issues;
- combining them avoids thin duplicate pages while retaining the distinctions that affect rehabilitation decisions.

Why it is classified under `adult-geriatric-rehabilitation`:
- the pathway is multisystem rather than cardiac or pulmonary;
- the category already covers multimorbidity, frailty, falls, prolonged hospitalization and preserving independence;
- the content emphasizes functional reserve, sarcopenia, aging, cognition, falls and community return.

Current high-value evidence:
- AAPM&R Liver and Kidney Transplant Rehabilitation;
- 2026 solid-organ prehabilitation/rehabilitation review;
- 2026 kidney-transplant exercise meta-analysis;
- liver-transplant exercise systematic review/meta-analysis;
- 2025 liver-transplant survivorship systematic review;
- broader solid-organ exercise meta-analysis.

## Evidence caution

The evidence supports structured physical activity/exercise and rehabilitation as useful components of post-transplant care, but the optimal exercise type and prescription are not established uniformly across organs and patient groups. Current kidney evidence includes low/very-low certainty for many outcomes. Pages therefore use individualized progressive rehabilitation rather than a fixed universal FITT prescription.
