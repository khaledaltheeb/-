# Transplant rehabilitation expansion — wave 6

Release date: 2026-09-06
Sector: `rehabilitation-functioning`
Production: https://healthrenewal.org

## Summary

Wave 6 extends the rehabilitation sector with two transplant-specific pathways that were not covered by the general cardiac, pulmonary or adult-rehabilitation guides:

1. `lung-transplant-rehabilitation-guide`
2. `liver-kidney-transplant-rehabilitation-guide`

Both pages were released through `private.content_release_gate_v6`, are published/indexable, and are linked to the central source registry.

## Lung-transplant rehabilitation

Category: `cardiopulmonary-rehabilitation`

Verified production state:
- published + indexable;
- 2,630 Arabic words;
- 7 central source links.

### Distinct value

The page covers:
- prehabilitation before lung transplantation;
- functional assessment and exercise tolerance;
- oxygen use within the transplant-center prescription;
- strength, mobility and nutrition before surgery;
- frailty assessment and intervention;
- early mobility after transplant;
- chest/shoulder mobility, pain and wound constraints;
- swallowing/voice issues after intubation and critical care;
- airway-clearance and respiratory-rehabilitation context;
- immunosuppression and infection-aware activity planning;
- graft/rejection-related functional change as a reason for medical reassessment, not a rehabilitation diagnosis;
- rehabilitation after infection, rejection treatment or readmission;
- bone health, falls and aging with a lung transplant;
- psychosocial recovery, work, travel and community participation;
- home/hybrid rehabilitation and long-term maintenance.

### Evidence anchors

- AAPM&R / PM&R KnowledgeNow: Pulmonary Rehabilitation Before and After Pulmonary Transplantation;
- ISHLT 2021 lung-transplant candidate consensus;
- ISHLT 2026 consensus on frailty in lung-transplant candidates;
- ISHLT international perioperative / intensive-care recommendations for lung transplantation;
- American Thoracic Society pulmonary-rehabilitation guidance;
- WHO Rehabilitation and ICF.

### Evidence boundary

Oxygen flow, listing/candidacy, postoperative restrictions and transplant-specific infection precautions remain under the responsible lung-transplant center. The page does not publish universal exercise or oxygen prescriptions.

## Liver and kidney transplant rehabilitation

Category: `adult-geriatric-rehabilitation`

Verified production state:
- published + indexable;
- 2,665 Arabic words;
- 8 central source links.

### Distinct value

The page covers:
- prehabilitation and maintenance of function while awaiting transplant;
- frailty and sarcopenia;
- dialysis-related activity constraints for kidney-transplant candidates;
- liver-disease-related weakness, nutrition and cognitive issues;
- early mobility and abdominal-wound recovery after transplant;
- progressive aerobic and resistance training;
- the current evidence base for kidney-transplant exercise;
- the smaller but supportive evidence base for liver-transplant exercise and survivorship interventions;
- immunosuppressive-medication effects on muscle, bone, glucose, blood pressure and nerves;
- infection-aware return to community activity;
- bone health and fall prevention;
- metabolic health and weight after transplant;
- cognition after hepatic encephalopathy;
- graft-protection considerations during sport and physical work;
- return to work, travel, medication routines and long-term rehabilitation re-entry.

### Evidence anchors

- AAPM&R / PM&R KnowledgeNow: Liver and Kidney Transplant Rehabilitation;
- 2026 review of evidence-based prehabilitation and rehabilitation in solid-organ transplantation;
- 2026 kidney-transplant exercise systematic review and meta-analysis;
- liver-transplant exercise systematic review/meta-analysis;
- 2025 liver-transplant survivorship-intervention systematic review;
- systematic review/meta-analysis of exercise across adult solid-organ transplant recipients;
- WHO Rehabilitation and ICF.

### Evidence boundary

The 2026 kidney-transplant exercise meta-analysis reports improvements in several functional/fitness outcomes but rates certainty as low or very low for many outcomes. The page therefore avoids presenting one exercise type, dose or intensity as universally optimal.
