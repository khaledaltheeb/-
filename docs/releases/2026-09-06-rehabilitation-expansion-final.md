## Evidence architecture

AAPM&R / PM&R KnowledgeNow served as a specialty anchor where appropriate, but pages were triangulated with current authoritative evidence families such as WHO, NICE, Canadian Stroke Best Practices, VA/DoD, INCOG, PVA/Consortium for Spinal Cord Medicine, SCIRE, ASIA, AHA/AACVPR, ATS, GOLD, ISHLT, AAOS, ACR, OARSI, NCI, ASCO, DEC/ECTA, NCPMI, UNICEF, ASHA, Jordan institutional sources, and rights-based sources where relevant.

The final gap-led phase also used:

- AAPM&R Cerebral Palsy plus existing AACPDM/CDC/WHO evidence;
- AAPM&R Pressure Injury Management in CNS Disorders updated June 2026;
- the Fourth Edition International Pressure Injury Living Guideline and current 2025–2026 modules;
- WHO Wheelchair Provision Guidelines.

## Rights boundary

The expansion does not republish protected instruments or proprietary worksheets merely because they are clinically useful.

Examples:

- ISNCSCI is explained and linked to official ASIA material rather than reproduced.
- MPOC 2.0 is referenced but its questionnaire is not copied.
- protected neuropsychological tests are not reproduced.
- pressure-injury classification photographs/diagrams and proprietary guideline artwork are not copied; the site links to the official international guideline instead.
- organization names indicate evidence provenance only and do not imply endorsement, accreditation, certification, or partnership.

## Legacy consistency repair

Older published pages discovered below the current standard were rebuilt rather than duplicated. Major upgrades include psychosocial rehabilitation, psychosis/work, ICF functioning, rehabilitation goal review, child safety/autonomy, family-centred rehabilitation, early-intervention motor and social-emotional routines, school transition, neuropsychological rehabilitation, Parkinson-capabilities synchronization, lifelong limb difference, and pressure-injury prevention.

The pressure-injury page is a particularly important example: it had enough legacy words but still contained repetitive template-like text. It was replaced with a current evidence-led pathway, raised to **2,789 words**, **10 central sources**, **8 claim mappings**, passed the actual V6 gate, and only then was cross-linked into rehabilitation.

## Editorial decision after this release

The rehabilitation program is now **gap-led and maintenance-led**, not quota-led.

The mandatory pre-publication sequence is:

1. search the whole site for existing coverage;
2. inspect the quality and current evidence of the best existing page;
3. enrich and cross-link that page when it can carry the topic cleanly;
4. create a new durable guide only when a distinct clinical, service-system, local/regional, professional, or research gap remains.

This prevents semantic duplication while allowing the rehabilitation taxonomy to become richer through high-quality cross-sector knowledge.
