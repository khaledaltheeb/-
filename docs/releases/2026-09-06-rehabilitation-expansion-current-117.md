## Evidence standard

The work uses AAPM&R / PM&R KnowledgeNow as a specialty anchor where useful, then triangulates with the strongest relevant contemporary guideline, consensus, systematic-review, or official source families. Examples include WHO, NICE, ACC/AHA, AHA/AACVPR, ATS, GOLD, ISHLT, VA/DoD, Canadian Stroke Best Practices, INCOG, PVA/SCIRE/ASIA, AAOS/ACR/OARSI, NCI/ASCO, Spina Bifida Association, AACPDM, DEC/ECTA/NCPMI, ASHA, UNICEF, Jordan institutional sources, and topic-specific international societies.

Recent 2025–2026 evidence has been used where it materially changes the page, including:

- 2026 pressure-injury Living Guideline modules;
- 2026 lung-transplant frailty consensus;
- 2026 SMA newborn-screening / early-treatment motor-trajectory consensus;
- 2026 DMD respiratory-rehabilitation consensus;
- 2026 return-to-sport reviews;
- 2026 PAD exercise network meta-analyses;
- 2026 VTE structured-exercise and post-PE evidence;
- 2025–2026 Jordan inclusion/education context.

## Rights and reuse policy

The expansion intentionally avoids reproducing protected clinical instruments and copyrighted guideline artwork.

Examples:

- ISNCSCI: explained and linked, not copied.
- MPOC 2.0: referenced, questionnaire not reproduced.
- protected neuropsychological tests: not reproduced.
- pressure-injury classification photographs/figures: not reproduced.
- organization names identify evidence provenance; they do not imply endorsement, accreditation, certification, or partnership.

## Operating rule from this point forward

Rehabilitation content is now **gap-led rather than quota-led**.

Before creating another page:

1. search the whole site, not just rehabilitation;
2. identify whether a strong existing page can be upgraded;
3. improve its evidence and V6 structure if necessary;
4. cross-link it rather than duplicate it when its canonical scope remains correct;
5. create a new page only for a genuinely distinct unmet pathway;
6. verify publication through the actual release gate.

This is the current operating standard for continued rehabilitation expansion.
